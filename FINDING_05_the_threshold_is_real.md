# SEVENFOLD / FINDING 05
## The threshold is scientifically defensible. That reframes the case rather than ending it.

**Status:** CONFIRMED from FDA's own scientific assessment, held as
`exhibits/fda_7oh_scientific_assessment.pdf` (649 KB, captured 2026-08-27).
**This finding cuts AGAINST the opening hypothesis and is recorded for that reason.**

---

### 1. Where the 0.05% line comes from

FDA's assessment, "7-Hydroxymitragynine (7-OH): An Assessment of the Scientific
Data and Toxicological Concerns Around an Emerging Opioid Threat", states:

> "In another analysis of 13 commercial products purported to contain kratom, the
> 7-OH content by weight ranged from **0.01-0.04%** (Kikura-Hanajiri et al., 2009)
> a finding in agreement with others that have reported 7-OH to account for
> **less than 0.05% by weight**, substantially lower than reported mitragynine
> amounts (Kruegel et al., 2019)."

And from a 341-sample study of consumers' own products:

> "the 7-OH content ... ranged from below the limit of quantitation (< 0.005%) to
> a **maximum of 0.21%** with a **mean of 0.01%** (Sharma et al., 2025)."

**The DEA threshold is set at the documented ceiling of naturally occurring 7-OH.**
It is not an arbitrary number and it is not reverse-engineered from a product
label. It has a citable basis in peer-reviewed literature predating this dispute
by up to seventeen years.

### 2. FDA on the pharmacology and the manufacturing

> "7-OH demonstrates substantially greater mu-opioid receptor potency than
> kratom's primary alkaloid constituent mitragynine, as well as other classical
> opioids such as morphine... 7-OH produces respiratory depression, physical
> dependence, and withdrawal symptoms characteristic of classical opioids."

> "The enhanced amount of 7-OH in these products is likely **synthetically
> derived through oxidate chemical conversion** of mitragynine isolates or kratom
> extracts. Given the trace amounts of 7-OH that are naturally present in kratom,
> direct extraction of 7-OH from plant material would simply be **unfeasible
> economically**."

**Corroborated independently by the patent record.** US **12,492,201 B1** (granted
2025-12-09) and US **12,466,830** (granted 2025-11-11), both titled "Method of
converting mitragynine to 7-hydroxymitragynine", claim industrial oxidation
processes yielding >=95% purity. Inventors: **Haywood Max Mitchell, Cecil Page,
Joseph William Merino**. Priority 2024-05-15.

**Lead:** FDA's warning letter of **2025-06-25** to **Thang Botanicals, Inc.
d/b/a 7OHMZ** is addressed to "**Mitchell** and Kumar". Same surname as the lead
inventor. If confirmed, the holder of the 7-OH manufacturing patent is also a
target of the enforcement action. **Not yet confirmed; the FDA letter URL 404s and
needs re-sourcing.**

### 3. What this does to the case

**Retire this claim.** That the 7-OH scheduling action was fabricated, or that its
threshold was drawn to protect Botanic Tonics. It is not supportable. A competent
opponent would dismantle it in one paragraph using FDA's own citations, and doing
so would discredit everything else in this file.

**These claims survive intact, and are stronger without the overreach:**

1. **The process was captured even if the outcome is sound.** A defensible rule and
   a corrupt process are not mutually exclusive. The officials who championed,
   announced and now administer this rule were funded by, met privately with, and
   in one case financially invested in, the commercial beneficiary.
2. **The disclosure failure is independent of the science.** Finding 01 stands on
   its own regardless of whether the threshold is correct.
3. **The warning removals have no scientific defense at all.** See below.

### 4. The part that has no defense

The scheduling action leaves **mitragynine** entirely unscheduled. Public Citizen
attributes **26 deaths over five years** to mitragynine specifically, and NYT
reports kratom present in **5,200+** overdose decedents 2020-2024.

Against that, per NYT: Mullin urged HHS to remove kratom-harm language from the
FDA website, as senator and then as DHS Secretary, and **by the end of 2025 FDA
had removed the links.** FDA refused a further request to delete a warning about
"liver toxicity, seizures and substance use disorder."

**[I] This, not the 7-OH ban, is the center of the case.**

Restricting a more dangerous competitor is coherent public health, whatever the
motive. Weakening consumer warnings on the product category you personally hold
$500,001-$1,000,000 of, which is the category carrying the documented deaths, is
not a policy position. There is no scientific literature that supports removing a
warning. And unlike the threshold, there is no expert who will defend it.
