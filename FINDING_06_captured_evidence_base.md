# SEVENFOLD / FINDING 06
## Industry money inside FDA's own scientific evidence base

**Status:** CONFIRMED on the journal's published disclosure statement and the LDA
corpus. One sequencing question remains open and is flagged below.
**Date:** 2026-08-27. **This finding does not depend on Mullin at all.**

### 1. The chain

FDA's assessment "7-Hydroxymitragynine (7-OH): An Assessment of the Scientific
Data and Toxicological Concerns Around an Emerging Opioid Threat" is the document
that carries the pharmacological case for scheduling 7-OH while leaving leaf
kratom alone. Held at `exhibits/fda_7oh_scientific_assessment.pdf`.

Among its citations:

> Huestis, M. A., Brett, M. A., Bothmer, J., & **Atallah, R.** (2024). Human
> Mitragynine and 7-Hydroxymitragynine Pharmacokinetics after Single and Multiple
> Daily Doses of Oral Encapsulated Dried Kratom Leaf Powder. *Molecules*, 29(5).

That paper's own disclosures state:

> "**Johnson Foods LLC paid for the clinical study** on its dried kratom leaf
> powder product MitraLeaf to be performed by SGS Nutrasource... **Huestis, Brett,
> Bothmer, and Atallah are paid consultants to Johnson Foods, LLC.** Atallah is
> also an employee of Della Terra Pharmaceuticals."

**Johnson Foods, LLC is a registered federal lobbying client of Holland & Knight.**

Buy the science, file the notification, lobby the regulator. Each step is lawful

### 2. What that means

The scientific record FDA relied on to justify a rule that removes leaf kratom's
competitors includes a human pharmacokinetics study:

- **paid for by** a leaf-kratom company that lobbies the federal government,
- **authored entirely by** that company's paid consultants,
- **one of whom** is the chief scientist of a second leaf-kratom company holding
  a sitting cabinet secretary's investment, and a consultant to the sector's
  largest lobbying organisation.

**[I]** This is a materially stronger and more portable finding than the Mullin
conflict, because it does not require anyone's motive to be established. It is
visible entirely in published disclosures. It also survives the Finding 05
concession: even if the 0.05% threshold is scientifically correct, the process
that assembled the supporting record was not independent of the interests it
benefits.

### 3. The caveat that must travel with this

The *Molecules* disclosure places Atallah at **Della Terra** and as a **Johnson
Foods consultant**. It does **not** list Botanic Tonics. Public traces place him
at Botanic Tonics by **December 2024**; the paper published **February 2024**.

**The Botanic Tonics role may therefore postdate the disclosure, in which case the
journal statement was accurate when written.** Do not write or imply that he
concealed an affiliation until the employment sequence is established. The
conflict of interest is real either way. The allegation of concealment is not yet
supported and must not be made.

### 4. A separate defect in the same FDA document

The assessment supports "**less than 0.05% by weight**", the figure that became
the legal threshold, with an in-text citation to "**(Kruegel et al., 2019)**".

**There is no Kruegel 2019 in the bibliography.** The reference list contains only
Kruegel et al. **2016** (J Am Chem Soc 138(21), 6754-6764, and as co-author on one
further entry). In-text counts: "Kruegel et al., 2016" twice, "Kruegel et al.,
2019" once, and the 2019 instance is the one carrying the threshold number.

**[I]** The number is independently supported by Kikura-Hanajiri et al. 2009
(0.01-0.04% by weight across 13 commercial products), so this is very likely a
citation error rather than a fabricated basis. But it is a dangling reference on
the single most consequential sentence in the document, and it is the sort of
defect that matters in a comment period or on judicial review.

### 7. Holland & Knight: tested, and what could not be tested

Prompted by a question about whether H&K's foreign-agent work connects to this
case, we ran the check rather than relying on the prior that large firms have many
clients.

**H&K's full FARA book is six foreign principals** [P]: Government of the Bahamas
(2022-04-26), Ministry of Trade Industry and Energy of South Korea (2022-02-04),
Embassy of Qatar via Moran Global Strategies (2020-02-20), **Israel Ministry of
Foreign Affairs (2019-01-09)**, Embassy of Japan (2015-05-08), Government of
Gibraltar (2014-05-14).

**The individual-level test could NOT be run.** The FARA foreign-principals
dataset contains only firm-to-principal relationships; its fields are registrant
name, principal name, country, dates and address. **It carries no individual agent
names.** Searching it for the four Johnson Foods lobbyists returned zero, but that
is a **structural null, not evidence of absence**, and must not be reported as a
negative. The data that would answer it is FARA **short-form registrations**, one
per individual agent, reachable only as documents rather than API records.

**[I] Standing analytical position:** shared outside counsel is not a link between
clients. Even confirmed individual overlap would show only that a firm monetises
both accounts. A genuine tie requires money or instruction moving between the
parties. Do not publish the firm-level overlap as a connection.
