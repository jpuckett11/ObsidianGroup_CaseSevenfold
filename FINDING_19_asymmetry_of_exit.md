# SEVENFOLD / FINDING 19
## The asymmetry of exit: why the harm falls on people with the fewest options

**Status:** analytical, built on findings established elsewhere in this file.
**Date:** 2026-08-27.

---

### 1. The controlled case

Jay Puckett's account (Finding 13) is not a typical exposure. It is close to
a best case, and that is precisely what makes it useful.

He went in with:

- **Knowledge.** He knew it was an opioid before he took the first tablet.
- **Intent.** He chose the moment, the product, and the purpose.
- **A physician.** Someone who would assess him and write a buprenorphine script.
- **Money.** For the medication and the visit.
- **Housing and a spouse.** A stable place to be ill in, and another adult present.
- **A planned exit.** He intended to stop and documented it as he went.

**With every one of those advantages, it took three weeks, 6,000 mg, and a
prescribed opioid-withdrawal medication to get out.** Physical dependence was
established within seven days in a man with no prior opioid dependence.

### 2. Now remove the advantages

That is the actual consumer. Consider what the same product does to someone
buying it off a shelf on a Tuesday:

| Puckett had | The ordinary purchaser has |
|---|---|
| Knew it was an opioid | A label reading "botanical supplementation" (Finding 16) |
| Knew the serving size | A 50 mg tablet whose labeled serving is one quarter of it |
| Knew dependence was coming | An addiction warning kept in a help-center article, off the product page |
| A physician who would see him | Often no appointment available, and no coverage |
| Money for buprenorphine | The cost of the script, or nothing |
| A spouse and a house | Frequently neither |
| A planned exit | No framework for what is happening, and one thing that makes it stop |

**[I] That last row is the mechanism.** A person in unexpected opioid withdrawal,
who has not been told what it is, has one immediately available intervention: buy
more. The product is its own remedy. That is not a failure of willpower. It is a
trap with the exits removed, and the removals were design decisions.

**[I] The people the harm concentrates on are not people with less character than
Puckett. They are the same trap without the money, the doctor, the
warning, or the spouse.**

### 3. What the record shows about who bears it

From FDA's own adverse event data (Finding 11), with the causation caveat stated:

- Largest affected age group: **25 to 34**, at 278 reports. Then 35 to 44 at 220.
- **Eleven reports involve a person under 18.**
- The most reported effect after death is **dependence**, at 256.

From the Tampa Bay Times (Finding 8): **580+ kratom-involved overdose deaths in
Florida alone**, 46 attributed to kratom alone, with over 90 percent since 2017.

These are working-age adults, and the dominant harm is addiction.

### 4. Who was on each side of the decision

Stated only as far as the documents carry it.

**Documented, from primary records:**

- Disclosed lobbying in this sector totals **$7,397,500**. The organized defense
  of the restricted product disclosed **$15,000**. (Finding 12)
- The trade association's model state bill was introduced in **34 states** and
  passed in **11**, with **seven of every ten words identical** between the
  Massachusetts and Kansas versions. (Finding 8)
- A **$980,000** lobbying vehicle with **no disclosed funder** lobbied DEA, DOJ,
  OMB and the Executive Office of the President, describing its subject in eight
  words. (Finding 14)
- The federal scientific assessment relies in part on a study **paid for by a
  $700,000 lobbying client** and written entirely by that client's paid
  consultants. (Finding 6)
- A serving Secretary of Homeland Security holds **$500,001 to $1,000,000** in a
  manufacturer in the product category he publicly advocated protecting, a holding
  that appears on **no divestiture list and in no congressional filing**.
  (Findings 1, 4)
- The same official **pressed HHS to remove kratom harm information from FDA web
  properties, and by the end of 2025 the links were removed.** A further request
  to delete a warning about liver toxicity, seizures and substance use disorder
  was declined. (Finding 3)

**Not documented, and not claimed:** what any official intended, or that any
particular decision was made because of a financial interest. **We do not assert
motive. The record above does not require it.**

**[I] What can be said without inference: consumers in this market had no
organized representation.** Seven vehicles and $7.4 million represented sellers of
one product class. One vehicle and $15,000 represented sellers of the other. Zero
dollars and zero vehicles represented the people becoming dependent. The only
consumer-facing safety information at issue was federal warning text, and that was
the one thing removed.

### 5. Why this belongs in every document

**[I]** It is the argument that survives every concession we have made. The 7-OH
scheduling action is defensible (Finding 5). The threshold has a real scientific
basis. Mitragynine's IND status is a legitimate reason not to schedule it
(Finding 11). We have withdrawn several of our own claims.

**None of that touches this.** Whatever the correct regulation of 7-OH turns out
to be, the people carrying the harm were sold an opioid described as a botanical,
were not told it was habit-forming at the point of sale, and had their federal
warning information reduced during the same period, while every organized party
in the fight was funded by someone selling something.
