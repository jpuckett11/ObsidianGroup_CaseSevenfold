### 2. The finding: Mitradyne Corporation claims the enzyme that makes it [P]

**EP 4 538 367 A2**, application 24201792.9, published **2025-04-16**. Canadian
sibling CA3254764A1. Priority **2023-09-20** from US provisional 63/583,976.
Applicant **Mitradyne Corporation, Toronto, Ontario**. Inventors Jose Casaretto
and Tariq A. Aktar, of Guelph, Ontario.

Title: *Polypeptides for use in the synthesis of kratom alkaloids.*

The compound appears **seventeen times**. It is not a list entry. Two of the
numbered claims are about nothing else. Verbatim:

> **Claim 5.** A polypeptide encoding a hydroxylase that hydroxylates the indole
> ring of corynantheidine at the C9 position to produce **9-hydroxycorynantheidine**.

> **Claim 6.** A polypeptide encoding a hydroxylase that hydroxylates the indole
> ring of mitragynine at the C7 position to produce **7-hydroxymitragynine**.

Read those two together. The same document, in consecutive claims, covers the
enzyme that makes the **scheduled** compound and the enzyme that makes the
**unscheduled** one. Whoever drafted this understood both positions on both
scaffolds in 2023.

> **Claim 8.** A polypeptide encoding an O-methyltransferase that methylates the
> hydroxyl group at the C9 position of 9-hydroxycorynantheidine to produce
> **mitragynine**.

> **Claim 11.** The system of claim 10, wherein the system is **cell-free**.

Specific enzyme sequences are claimed at SEQ ID NO: 23-54 for the hydroxylases and
55-65 for the methyltransferase.

### 3. The mechanism detail that matters most [P, from the claims]

Claim 8 states the biosynthetic order. Written out:

    corynantheidine --(C9 hydroxylase)--> 9-hydroxycorynantheidine
                    --(O-methyltransferase)--> mitragynine

**9-hydroxycorynantheidine is an intermediate on the route to mitragynine.** It is
not an exotic derivative someone would have to set out to make. It is a mandatory
waypoint. Any facility producing mitragynine by this enzymatic route passes
through it, and can stop there and isolate it.

That is a materially different risk profile from a compound requiring bespoke
synthesis, and it is the single most important sentence in this finding.

### 4. A second, independent platform [P]

**US 2025/0179544 A1**, application **18/965,429**, priority **2023-12-01**.
Applicant **CB Therapeutics, Inc., Carlsbad, California**.

Title: *Production of corynanthe-type monoterpene indole alkaloid compounds in a
recombinant host.*

Where Mitradyne is cell-free enzymes, this is **recombinant cells**: fermentation.
Its claims run to "a recombinant cell comprising a polynucleotide..." and its
specification lists the products, OCR'd from the filing:

> "...demethylcorynantheidine (20R, 20S), 9-hydroxydemethylcorynantheidine,
> 9-methoxydemethylcorynantheidine, corynantheidine, dihydrocorynantheine,
> **9-hydroxycorynantheidine**, hydroxydihydrocorynantheine, speciogynine, and/or
> mitragynine, among others."

**Who CB Therapeutics is [P, SEC]:** Delaware corporation, incorporated
2015-03-20, CIK 1949369. Form C filed 2023-02-27 through OpenDeal Portal LLC,
seeking $25,000 to $1,235,000; **withdrawn 2023-04-11** (Form C-W). Eighteen
employees. Most recent fiscal year: revenue $2,014,066, net loss $1,772,158, total
assets $3,722,376.

A small company, not a major pharmaceutical firm. Note the identity check: a
separate **CB THERAPEUTICS INC. of Grand Cayman**, CIK 1681660, filed a Form D in
2016. Different address, different CIK. We have **not** established they are
related and have not treated them as the same entity.

**Mitradyne Corporation has no SEC filings.** It is Canadian and its corporate and
funding records were not obtained. That remains open.

### 5. Why the plant no longer matters

Both routes remove the botanical supply chain. No Indonesian farm, no import
declared as fertiliser, no leaf to test at the border, no alkaloid ratio dictated
by growing conditions. A bioreactor and a purification train produce a defined
compound at a defined purity.

Every enforcement assumption in this case rests on a plant crossing a border. Both
of these filings are about not needing one.

### 6. The precedent this repeats

Finding 20 recorded that **a granted process patent for 7-OH existed before
enforcement arrived**. The order was: patent, then product, then a market, then a
scheduling action years late.

On the corynantheidine scaffold the first step of that sequence is complete, on
two independent technology platforms, with priority dates in **September and
December 2023**. There is no product and no market yet.

The federal record still contains **zero** assessments of this compound
(Finding 20 s3, with controls). Florida named it in an emergency rule in
June 2026 (Finding 20 s4).

### 7. What we do not assert

- **No evidence** that either company intends to market 9-hydroxycorynantheidine
  as a consumer product. Nothing in either filing says so. Both describe platforms
  for producing kratom alkaloids generally, with mitragynine as the named target.
- Patents are filed years ahead of products and **most are never commercialised**.
  A claim is a claim, not a factory.
- **No evidence** connecting either company to the 7-OH scheduling action, to
  Botanic Tonics, to MIT45, to the American Kratom Association, or to any official
  named anywhere in this case. We looked and found nothing. There is no political
  money on this compound (Finding 20 s6), and this finding does not change that.
- Both are legitimate biotechnology filings. Publishing them is not an accusation.

### 8. What it changes

Finding 20 said the compound is "ahead of the money." That is still true of
political money. It is **no longer true of industrial capability**.

The corrected statement is: *the capacity to manufacture this compound at scale,
without a plant, has been claimed in two patent families since late 2023, while
the federal government has never assessed it.*

That belongs in the third comment to HHS-OASH-2026-0232 before the 2026-09-10
deadline, and the comment has been updated to carry it.

### B. Their published method is the patent's method [P]

Read the first title again. The group's own 2026 paper is **cell-free synthesis**
of a plant natural product. Claim 11 of EP4538367 is *"wherein the system is
cell-free."*

This is not a pharmaceutical company that decided to enter kratom. It is an
academic plant-biochemistry group that spent years building a cell-free platform
for making cannabis secondary metabolites, and then pointed the same platform at
*Mitragyna speciosa*. That is a coherent scientific lineage, and it explains how a
company with no visible commercial footprint produced a filing this specific.

The European agent of record is **Dehns, 10 Old Bailey, London** [P].

### E. What this does and does not mean

**[I]** A company whose registered office is in Toronto while all four of its
inventors work in Guelph and Kitchener, roughly a hundred kilometres away, with no
public funding, no web presence and no SEC trail, is most consistent with an
early-stage university-adjacent venture holding intellectual property. That is an
ordinary and lawful structure. Thousands of them exist.

**It is not evidence of concealment**, and we do not present it as such. We record
it because Finding 25 asserts that industrial capability for this compound has
been claimed, and a reader is entitled to know exactly how much is known about who
claimed it: four named academic scientists at a public university, a London patent
agent, and a Toronto address we could not resolve to a filing.

**Still open:** the Ontario registration, its directors and incorporation date;
whether the University of Guelph holds any interest through its commercialisation
office; and whether Mitradyne has raised capital.
