# SEVENFOLD / FINDING 27
## The uncovered compound is a major human metabolite of the covered one

**Status:** CONFIRMED from peer-reviewed pharmacokinetic literature.
**Analyst:** Aegis, for Obsidian Watch Group. **Date:** 2026-08-28.
**This finding materially corrects how Findings 20, 25 and 26 and our own filed
comments describe this compound.**

---

### 1. What we said, and why it was too weak

Comment `mtc-q9zv-dase`, filed 2026-08-28, describes 9-hydroxycorynantheidine as:

> "a minor indole alkaloid of *Mitragyna speciosa*, under one percent of total
> alkaloid content"

That is true of the **plant** and it badly understates the compound. It is not
principally something you consume. **It is something you produce.**

### 2. Human data [P]

**Kanumuri et al. (2026), *Pharmaceutical Biology*, DOI 10.1080/13880209.2026.2715806.**
UPLC-MS/MS method validated to FDA guidelines, applied to plasma from a clinical
trial of four regular kratom users:

> "Mitragynine, speciociliatine, mitraciliatine, isopaynantheine, and paynantheine
> are major circulating alkaloids, with mitragynine 16-carboxylic acid and
> **9-hydroxycorynantheidine being the major circulating metabolites of
> mitragynine** in plasma upon kratom oral administration."

The same paper groups it with the scheduled compound on stability:

> "Chemically unstable metabolites, **7-hydroxymitragynine and
> 9-hydroxycorynantheidine**, were stabilized in human plasma."

**In humans, 9-hydroxycorynantheidine is one of the two major circulating
metabolites of mitragynine.** Anyone who takes kratom produces it.

### 3. Animal data, labelled as such [P, rat]

**Chiang et al. (2025), *ACS Pharmacology & Translational Science* 7:2452-2464,
DOI 10.1021/acsptsci.4c00277.** Multiple-dose steady-state PK, male and female
rats:

> "Both sexes produced similar metabolite profiles; the major metabolites were
> mitragynine acid and **9-hydroxycorynantheidine**.
> **7-Hydroxymitragynine was a minor metabolite.**"

**This is a rat study and must be cited as one.** It is not evidence about humans
and this finding does not present it as such. What it establishes is that in a
mammalian model at steady state, the compound the government is scheduling is the
*minor* metabolite and the compound it is not scheduling is the *major* one.

**We flag the species limit ourselves** because the alternative is having it
flagged for us.

### 4. The mechanism, stated correctly

Mitragynine carries a **methoxy group at C9**. Removing that methyl gives the
9-hydroxy compound. This is **O-demethylation**, an ordinary cytochrome P450
reaction.

    mitragynine  --(O-demethylation at C9)-->  9-hydroxycorynantheidine

7-OH is a different reaction at a different position: **hydroxylation at C7** on
the intact molecule.

**It is not ring cleavage.** Nothing is lost from the ring system. Anyone
describing this publicly should say "the 9-methoxy is demethylated", because a
chemist will stop reading at "loses a ring" and the underlying point is strong
enough not to need looseness.

### 5. The collision with Finding 25

Mitradyne's patent claim 8 describes the **plant enzyme** running this reaction
forward:

> "an O-methyltransferase that methylates the hydroxyl group at the C9 position of
> **9-hydroxycorynantheidine to produce mitragynine**"

The clinical literature describes the **human body** running it backward.

    plant:  9-hydroxycorynantheidine  --(O-methyltransferase)-->  mitragynine
    human:  mitragynine  --(O-demethylation)-->  9-hydroxycorynantheidine

The same bond, in both directions. The plant builds mitragynine out of this
compound. The person taking it takes mitragynine apart and gets the compound back.

### 6. Why this changes the scheduling argument

The stated basis for controlling 7-OH is that it is a potent metabolite of
mitragynine formed in the body.

**9-hydroxycorynantheidine is the other major metabolite of the same parent, by
the same logic, and no federal instrument mentions it.** In the rat model it is
the larger of the two.

Corynantheidine, its parent scaffold, is a **mu-opioid receptor partial agonist**
with demonstrated MOR-dependent antinociception in mice (Kruegel-lineage work,
*Kratom Alkaloids as Probes for Opioid Receptor Function*, 2021). This is not an
inert molecule.

**The gap is no longer hypothetical and no longer depends on a future product.**
It exists in the bloodstream of every current kratom consumer, right now, and the
rule does not name it.

### 7. What we still do not assert

- **No human study reviewed establishes that 9-hydroxycorynantheidine exceeds
  7-OH in humans.** That comparison is available only in rats and is cited as rat
  data.
- No potency equivalence to 7-OH is claimed. Reported mu affinity for the parent
  scaffold is weaker.
- **No product containing it has been located for sale.** An unverified
  second-hand report of a retail purchase was received 2026-08-28 and is recorded
  as a lead in `LEADS.md`, not as a finding. It is not relied on here and this
  finding stands without it.
- We do not assert the omission was deliberate. **The simplest explanation is that
  scope was drawn around a scaffold rather than around metabolism**, which is
  precisely what our comments ask the Department to confirm or deny.
