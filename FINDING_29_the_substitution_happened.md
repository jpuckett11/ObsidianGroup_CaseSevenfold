# SEVENFOLD / FINDING 29
## The substitution already happened. It is being sold as "Cat's Claw," and it has killed someone.

**Status:** CONFIRMED from a state poison control alert, a state agriculture
department warning, and contemporaneous reporting.
**Analyst:** Aegis, for Obsidian Watch Group. **Date:** 2026-08-28.
**This finding CORRECTS Finding 28, which was wrong.**

---

### 1. Correction first

Finding 28, published earlier the same day, concluded: *"there is no cat's claw
problem and this case does not claim there is one."*

**That conclusion was wrong.** The error is set out in full in Section 6, because
how we got it wrong matters as much as the correction.

### 4. And the other half is not adulteration at all [S, primary-adjacent]

A recovery community, `quitting7oh.org`, maintains a dedicated compound page:

> "Products marketed as **'Cat's Claw' or 'Rhynchophylline'** at smoke shops and
> gas stations are being **built around corynoxine B, an opioid-active alkaloid
> that dodges the 7-OH scheduling**."

**Read that against Finding 28.** We independently established from *ACS Chemical
Neuroscience* 2021 that corynoxine is a **mu-opioid receptor full agonist**. The
people in withdrawal from these products identified the same compound family from
the other end, without a literature search, because they were living it.

**These are two distinct phenomena and both are real:**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Adulteration** | "Cat's Claw" branded products spiked with scheduled 7-OH, MGM-15 and related. Illegal. A death in Utah |
| **Substitution** | Products genuinely built on corynoxine B, an opioid-active alkaloid **no federal instrument covers**. Currently lawful |

The second is exactly what comments `mtc-q9zv-dase` and `mtd-holx-p7i5` warned the
Department about. **It is not a forecast. It is on shelves.**

### 6. How we got it wrong, recorded so it is not repeated

Three failures, in order of seriousness.

**a. We measured the plant instead of the product.** Finding 28 computed corynoxine
content in *Uncaria tomentosa* bark and concluded the exposure was small. That was
arithmetic about a botanical. **The products in question are not that botanical.**
They are smoke-shop tablets using the name. Measuring the plant to draw conclusions
about the product was a category error, and it is the same class of mistake as an
earlier entity mix-up in this case that was caught by checking rather than
assuming.

**b. We treated absence in CAERS as absence of harm.** Nine adverse event reports
against 879 for kratom, with no dependence signal, was read as reassurance. **CAERS
is a passive system.** It records people who call a poison centre. It does not
record a dependent person who does not call anyone. **The investigator made this
objection immediately and was right**, from direct personal knowledge of what
dependence on these products looks like from the inside.

**c. We did not ask whether the name was being used as a product name.** The
question "is *Uncaria tomentosa* dangerous" was answered. The question "**what is
being sold under the words cat's claw**" was never asked, and it was the only one
that mattered. It took one search.

**The finding was published with confident reassurance for roughly one hour.** It
is corrected here, in public, with the reasoning intact, because a case that
publishes its own errors is the only kind worth reading.
