### 1. What this case has argued, and what the seller says

Five comments to HHS-OASH-2026-0232 argue that scope drawn around named molecules
invites substitution onto uncovered ones.

**The manufacturer states that this is what the product is.** Not as an accusation
we make. As marketing, on the front page, in the first person.

### 2. The seller's own words [P, `exhibits/marketing/buzzers/`]

From **getbuzzers.com**, homepage banner:

> "Buzzers Cat's Claw Extract Products **No 7-OH or Kratom. Legal in all 50
> states.**"

From the same page, "What Is Cat's Claw?":

> "After much research, **we figured out a way to extract an indole alkaloid from
> the Cat's Claw plant to mirror the effects of popular kratom products.** We're
> able to get so close because, **despite not using kratom, we're still producing a
> very similar indole alkaloid.** The results are a **non-kratom product with
> kratom-like effects.**"

From a retailer page, **thegreendragoncbd.com**, stating the legal rationale
outright:

> "**Legal in all 50 states: Cat's Claw is a recognized botanical and is not a
> controlled substance, so Buzzers ship nationwide.**"

From **exoclub.com**:

> "These potent extracts pack a punch and **DON'T CONTAIN any Kratom or popular
> Kratom compounds.** ... We extract an indole alkaloid from the Cat's Claw plant
> to mirror the effects of popular kratom products. **Users should feel something
> extremely similar to their preferred** [product]."

**Read together, that is a written statement that the product is engineered to
reproduce the effects of a substance being scheduled, using a molecule that is
not.** It is the definition of regulatory arbitrage, published by the party doing
it.

### 3. It is sold as a stimulant

The marketing is uniformly about **energy and focus**, not sedation or analgesia:

> "a smooth, uplifting experience... **clarity, focus, and high-functioning
> energy**... Rather than overstimulation, the formula promotes a balanced and
> productive vibe."

A customer testimonial carried on the manufacturer's own homepage:

> "**if you like kratom you'll love these.** They give you energy but it's a very
> mellow and content feeling. I take one in the morning and I'm ready for [the
> day]."

**A buyer reading that page would understand they are buying a focus supplement.**

### 4. What a state health authority says it actually is

Utah Poison Control Center, 22 July 2026, naming this brand:

> "may lead to **life-threatening opioid overdose, dependence, or withdrawal**...
> These include **'Buzzers' and 'Homiez'** brand products... **multiple reports of
> severe overdose**... adulterated with **7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH), mitragynine
> pseudoindoxyl, MGM-15, or MGM-16**... **more potent than morphine**... The
> product labels are misleading and **do not include these drugs as ingredients**."

A **death** in Utah County was linked to mislabelled Buzzers-brand Cat's Claw
tablets on 30 July 2026, with **MGM-15** identified.

**Sold as an upper. Killing people as an opioid.**

### 5. Both brands the state named are the same operation [P]

The Utah alert names **"Buzzers" and "Homiez"** as separate brands.
**getbuzzers.com sells both**, carrying a product line called **"Buzzin with The
Homiez"** in its own navigation.

### 6. The "legal in all 50 states" claim appears to be false

Utah **banned kratom products other than pure-leaf** in its 2026 legislative
session, and MGM-15 is a **Schedule I drug under Utah law**. A product found to
contain MGM-15 is not lawful in Utah, and the state agriculture department ordered
retailers to remove it.

**"Legal in all 50 states" is a specific, checkable, prominent claim**, and on the
present record it is not accurate.

### 7. Why this matters more than anything else in the marketing file

The FTC complaint (`deliverables/ftc/`) rests on a contradiction between two pages
on one domain: "botanical supplementation" on the sales page, "habit-forming" in
the help center.

**This is stronger.** Here the seller is not contradicting itself by accident. It
is **explaining its method**: extract a similar molecule from a different plant,
reproduce the effect, avoid the schedule, ship everywhere.

**No inference is required and no motive needs to be assumed.** It is on the
homepage.

### 8. What we do not assert

- **We have not assayed any Buzzers product.** We do not know what is in a tablet.
  The adulteration finding is the Utah Poison Control Center's, and is theirs to
  stand behind.
- **We do not assert the manufacturer knowingly adulterated anything.** The alert
  says "we suspect." A supply chain can be contaminated upstream of a brand.
- **We do not assert that corynoxine B is in this product.** The recovery-community
  attribution (Finding 29) is not a laboratory result.
- **We assert only what the pages say**, which is captured, hashed and dated.
