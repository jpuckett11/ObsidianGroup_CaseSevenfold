# SEVENFOLD / FINDING 33
## The recovery field showed up on this docket, and it is split down the middle

**Status:** CONFIRMED from comments on HHS-OASH-2026-0232.
**Analyst:** Aegis, for Obsidian Watch Group. **Date:** 2026-08-29.

---

### 1. Two comments, same harm, opposite asks

**`HHS-OASH-2026-0232-7588`** — **Awakening Recovery, Inc.**, filed 2026-07-16.
An organisation, named on the record:

> "We see some our residents, especially in the last year, come into **Awakening
> Recovery, a nonprofit with recovery homes for men and women**, having **overdosed
> on 7-Hydroxmitragynine**. Moving this to Schedule 1 would save lives. I hope you
> do this."

**`HHS-OASH-2026-0232-18012`** — filed as **"A, R"**, initials only, 2026-07-24.
A working clinician or recovery professional:

> "Every day I work with people rebuilding their lives after substance use
> disorders. One lesson is consistently reinforced: **recovery is fostered through
> treatment, stability, and human connection—not through expanding criminal
> penalties.** Before placing additional substances into Schedule I, I urge the DEA
> to weigh the lived experiences of clinicians, recovery professionals, and people
> in recovery who have witnessed the limitations of punitive drug policy. **Public
> health outcomes improve when policy emphasizes care over punishment.**"

**One runs recovery homes and is watching residents overdose on this compound. One
works with people rebuilding their lives and is warning that scheduling will not
help them.**

Both are describing the same population. **Neither is wrong.** That tension is the
honest state of this question and this case will not pretend it away.

### 2. On the second commenter, and a boundary this case is keeping

She filed under **initials only**, with no organisation, no city and no state. A
recovery professional commenting publicly on federal drug policy has obvious reasons
for that.

**We have not attempted to identify her and will not.** Her comment is a public
record with a permanent ID and is quoted here in full because that is what it was
filed for. **Her identity is not ours to establish.**

### 3. Her framing is nearly as rare as our own

Word counts across all 27,053 comments, single rare words only, nonsense-string
control returning 0:

| Term | Comments |
|---|---|
| "punitive" | **13** |
| "decriminalization" | 16 |
| "criminalization" | 2,650 |

**Thirteen.** Out of twenty-seven thousand. Her argument, that policy should
emphasise care over punishment, is made in roughly **0.05%** of the record.

The 2,650 "criminalization" comments are largely a different argument: kratom
consumers objecting to losing access. **That is a case for the product. Hers is a
case for the patient**, and almost nobody else made it.

### 4. Why this matters to `REMEDIATION.md`

That document argues, at length and with citations, that scheduling molecules does
not reduce demand and that the only durable intervention is treating people.

**A working recovery professional filed the same argument on the same docket, six
weeks before we did, in four sentences.** We reached it from federal filings,
patents and pharmacokinetics. She reached it from the room.

**She is corroboration, and she got there first.** Recorded here so the case does
not present the argument as its own discovery.

### 5. What this changes about Finding 26

Finding 26 establishes that **two questions** were unique to this case in the whole
docket: the corynantheidine scaffold gap and the phantom "(Kruegel et al., 2019)"
citation. **That remains true and is unaffected.**

**But the remediation argument is not ours alone**, and Finding 26 should not be
read as implying otherwise. Others reached the care-over-punishment conclusion
independently. **We were not first and do not claim to be.**
