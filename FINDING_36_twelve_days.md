# SEVENFOLD / FINDING 36
## The government won. Twelve days later it walked away.

**Status:** The 10 December 2025 order is **CONFIRMED [P]**, obtained and hashed.
The 22 December 2025 dismissal is **[S]**, resting on the claimant's own press
release, and is labelled as such throughout.
**Analyst:** Aegis, for Obsidian Watch Group. **Date:** 2026-08-29.
**This finding supplies the primary document that `FINDING_03` recorded as blocked**,
and it materially changes what the DOJ FOIA should ask.

## 1. What was already in the case, and what was missing

`FINDING_03` records, at `[S]`, that on **2025-12-22** the government filed a Notice
of Dismissal without prejudice in *United States v. 250,000 Filled Bottles of Liquid
Product*, 4:23-cv-00168 (N.D. Okla.). Its own open-items section said:

> "CourtListener API now requires a token. Docket pulls for both cases are blocked,
> not empty. **Need a token to retrieve the dismissal filing itself.**"

That block cleared on 2026-08-28. What came out of it was not the dismissal. **It was
the order twelve days before it, which nobody in this case had seen.**

## 2. The order [P]

**Document 33, filed 10 December 2025**, `OPINION AND ORDER`, obtained from the RECAP
archive and hashed into `exhibits/litigation/`.

> "Before the Court is a Motion to Dismiss the Government's First Amended Verified
> Complaint ("Motion") filed by Claimant Botanic Tonics, LLC. Dkt. No. 12. For the
> following reasons, **Botanic Tonics' Motion [Dkt. No. 12] is DENIED.**"

The court's reasoning, in its own words:

> "Most importantly, the Court finds that these detailed facts support a **reasonable
> belief that, if proven, the Government would be able to successfully meet its
> burden.** For these reasons, **the Amended Complaint survives**, and Botanic Tonic's
> Motion must be denied."

Signed **Gustavo A. Gelpí, United States Circuit Judge, sitting by designation.** The
caption changed mid-case from `4:23-cv-00168-JFH-CDL` to `-GAG-CDL`; the case began
before Chief Judge John F. Heil III and was decided by a First Circuit judge.

**Botanic Tonics had moved to dismiss on 17 May 2023. It was denied on 10 December
2025. That motion was pending for two years and seven months.**

## 4. What the United States actually alleged, and it was not 7-OH [P]

This is the part that matters most and it has not been in this case until now. From
the **operative amended complaint**, document 9:

> "17. The Defendant Articles are adulterated within the meaning of 21 U.S.C.
> § 342(f)(1)(B) in that they contain or are a new dietary ingredient, **kratom**, for
> which there is inadequate information to provide reasonable assurance that this
> ingredient does not present a significant or unreasonable risk of illness of
> injury."

> "12. **Serious safety concerns exist regarding the effect of kratom on multiple
> organ systems.** Consumption of kratom can lead to a number of negative health
> impacts, including respiratory depression, vomiting, nervousness, weight loss, and
> constipation. Kratom consumption has been linked to neurologic, analgesic and
> sedative effects, **addiction, and liver toxicity.**"

> "13. Mitragynine, the major alkaloid identified in kratom, has been reported as a
> **partial opioid agonist, producing effects that are similar to morphine.**
> Furthermore, a minor alkaloid of kratom, 7-hydroxymitragynine, has been reported to
> be **even more potent than morphine.**"

**The defendant articles were leaf products.** 250,000 bottles of *feel free* Plant
Based Herbal Supplement, 1,200 cartons of capsules, 1.5 tanks of liquid, and bulk
kratom powder labelled "Green Nano," at 13105 East 61st Street, Broken Arrow,
Oklahoma.

**In 2023 the United States told a federal court that kratom leaf itself was
adulterated, citing addiction and liver toxicity. In December 2025 a federal judge
held that claim viable. In July 2026 the federal rule scheduled 7-OH and expressly
excluded the leaf.**

## 5. The claimant's framing became the rule's structure [I]

Botanic Tonics' press release of 6 January 2026, captured and hashed:

> "The government filed a **Notice of Dismissal on December 22, 2025**, formally
> closing the matter. The dismissal reflects a maturing regulatory landscape in which
> federal agencies increasingly recognize the **fundamental differences between
> natural kratom leaf** ... **and concentrated synthetic kratom compounds manufactured
> in laboratories.**"

> "This outcome **validates what we've maintained from the beginning** ... enforcement
> resources are now appropriately focused on products that present legitimate public
> health concerns."

**[I] Inference, labelled as such.** The distinction the company announced in January
2026 as the meaning of its dismissal is the distinction the federal rule adopted in
July 2026: leaf on one side, 7-OH on the other. `FINDING_03` had already flagged this
as an inference from the press release alone. **It is now an inference standing on
the government's own abandoned pleading**, which had alleged the opposite about the
leaf.

## 6. This complicates our own thesis, and that is why it is here

Much of this case argues that the government does not act. **On this record it did.**
It investigated from 2022, seized roughly $3 million of product, pleaded a
substantive FDCA theory, opposed the motion to dismiss, and won.

**The failure is not inaction. It is worse than inaction.** An agency that never acts
has, at least, never told a court that a product injures people. This one did, for
two and a half years, prevailed, and then reportedly let it go without prejudice
twelve days later, at which point the claimant announced the abandonment as
scientific vindication and the rule that followed drew the line where the claimant
said it should be.

**We do not allege that anyone was instructed to drop it. We have no evidence of
that and we do not claim it.** What the record shows is a sequence and a
twelve-day interval.

## 7. What is NOT established

- **The dismissal itself is `[S]`.** Our only source that it happened, and that it was
  22 December 2025, is the claimant's press release. **A company describing the
  outcome of its own case is not a primary source for that outcome.**
- **CourtListener does not have it.** Verified with an authenticated API pull on
  2026-08-29: 31 entries returned, highest entry number 33, **nothing after the
  10 December order.** `date_terminated` is null and `date_last_filing` reads
  2024-10-15, both of which are stale against an entry dated December 2025, so the
  archive is demonstrably incomplete and its silence proves nothing either way.
- **Entries 31 and 32 are missing** from CourtListener entirely. We do not know what
  they were. They fall in the gap between December 2023 and December 2025.
- **No reason for the dismissal is known.** Not from the docket, not from the
  government, not from anything we hold.
- The original complaint, document 2, is a **scanned image with no text layer** and
  has not been OCR'd. The amended complaint supersedes it and is fully readable.

## 8. Correction to an earlier reading, recorded

On first pulling the docket this session, Aegis reported that the case had **not**
been dismissed, reasoning from the absence of any dismissal entry and the absence of
the words "dismissed," "voluntary," "Rule 41" and "terminated" anywhere in the docket
text.

**That was wrong, and the error was over-reading a silent archive as a negative
finding.** RECAP holds only what someone has uploaded. `FINDING_03` had it right at
`[S]` and had correctly labelled the retrieval as blocked rather than resolved. The
correction was made within minutes and before anything was filed or published, but it
is recorded because this case records them.

## 9. What this does to the DOJ FOIA

`FILINGS.md` READY §2 was drafted to ask **why the United States dismissed its own
forfeiture action.** That question is now much sharper and should be re-drafted before
filing:

**Why did the United States move to dismiss twelve days after prevailing on a
contested motion to dismiss, in an action it had litigated for two years and seven
months, having alleged that the seized articles were adulterated and injurious?**

The FOIA should now cite **document 33 by date and docket number**. A request that
quotes the court's own finding that the government would likely meet its burden is
considerably harder to answer with a boilerplate release.
