## 1. The case [P]

***Botanic Tonics, LLC v. Kelly Pherson, et al.***, No. **2:26-cv-267-HCN-DBP**
(D. Utah). Memorandum Decision and Order, document 28, filed **4 May 2026**, Judge
**Howard C. Nielson, Jr.**

> "Plaintiffs **Botanic Tonics, LLC, and Global Kratom Coalition, Inc.**, sue various
> Utah state officials seeking to enjoin enforcement of portions of Utah's newly
> enacted **Kratom Regulation Act**, which will otherwise take effect on May 6, 2026.
> The Plaintiffs request a preliminary injunction. **The court denies that request.**"

## 2. Who was sued [P]

Named defendants, all Utah state officials sued in connection with their offices:

| Name | Office |
|---|---|
| **Kelly Pherson** | Commissioner, **Utah Department of Agriculture and Food** |
| **Amber Brown** | Deputy Commissioner, **Utah Department of Agriculture and Food** |
| **Bradon Forsyth** | Director, Utah Specialized Product Division |
| **Derek Brown** | **Utah Attorney General** |

**Read that first row against Finding 29.** The Utah Department of Agriculture and
Food is the agency that, on **30 July 2026**, issued the public warning over
mislabeled Buzzers-brand "Cat's Claw" tablets containing MGM-15, in the matter where
a Utah County death was reported.

**The company sued that department's Commissioner and Deputy Commissioner on 31 March
2026. Four months later the same department was warning the public that people were
dying from products in the category.**

We are not alleging the suit caused the death or delayed the warning. **We have no
evidence of either and we do not claim it.** What the record shows is that the
regulator issuing that warning was, at the time, a named defendant in federal
litigation brought by the largest company in the category.

## 3. What the court held [P]

The plaintiffs argued **federal preemption**. The court applied the presumption
against it:

> "[I]n all pre-emption cases, and particularly in those in which Congress has
> legislated in a field which the States have traditionally occupied, [courts] start
> with the assumption that **the historic police powers of the States were not to be
> superseded** by the Federal Act unless that was the clear and manifest purpose of
> Congress." *Wyeth v. Levine*, 555 U.S. 555, 565 (2009).

> "For the foregoing reasons, Docket Number 2, Plaintiffs' **Motion for Preliminary
> Injunction, is DENIED.**"

**A state may regulate this category more tightly than the federal government does.**
That holding matters well beyond Utah, and it is the answer to the argument that
federal action settles the question.

## 4. What the filing revealed about scale [P]

From the court's recitation of the plaintiffs' own evidence:

- *feel free* is **"a combination kratom leaf and noble kava root dietary
  supplement."**
- It was **sold in 321 Utah stores.**
- Botanic Tonics' CEO averred the company had, until recently, a deal to place *feel
  free* in **an additional 852 retail store locations nationwide.**

**The Utah Act reaches it because it is a combination product**, not because of any
7-OH threshold. The court said so directly:

> "**feel free plainly does not qualify as pure leaf kratom because it is mixed with
> kava root**"

## 5. The compound Finding 29 named, appearing here [P]

The court declined to reach one argument, and the argument itself is the interesting
part:

> "the court need not address the Plaintiffs' argument that the possible presence of
> **trace amounts of mitragynine pseudoindoxyl** in pure leaf kratom and the
> scheduling of this substance would potentially bar the sale of pure leaf kratom"

**Mitragynine pseudoindoxyl is one of the four compounds Utah Poison Control named**
in its 22 July 2026 alert (Finding 29). Utah had scheduled it. The industry argued in
March 2026 that scheduling it went too far. In July 2026 the state's poison center
listed it among the substances it suspected in products causing severe overdoses.

## 7. What this does to the case's argument

Finding 34 said Congress acts only where action is free. Finding 35 said the useful
federal instrument was capped below its effective dose for a generation. **This
finding adds the part that was missing: where a government did act with teeth, it was
a state, and it got sued for it.**

That is a better explanation than indifference. **Federal inaction and state action
under litigation pressure are the same picture from two angles.** A state that bans a
combination product absorbs the legal cost directly. A federal agency that schedules
one molecule and exempts the leaf absorbs none.

**[I] Inference, labeled.** We do not claim the litigation caused the federal line to
be drawn where it was. We observe that the line the federal rule drew in July 2026 is
the line the company had been litigating for since March 2026 and announcing since
January 2026, and that the states drawing a different line are the ones being sued.

## 8. What is NOT established

  the briefing, or anything in the West Virginia matter.** The § 1983 characterization
  comes from the docket's cause of action field, not from the pleading.
- **We do not know the outcome of the Tenth Circuit appeal.** It was live as of
  2026-07-29.
- **We do not know why the earlier Utah case (2:25-cv-00886) terminated on
  2025-12-10**, which happens to be the same day as the forfeiture order in Finding
  36. **We note the coincidence of date and draw nothing from it.**
- No connection between this litigation and the Buzzers deaths is asserted. The
  products in Finding 29 are **not** Botanic Tonics products.
