# SEVENFOLD / FINDING 41
## The industry is not winning on preemption. It is winning where states wrote protectionist laws.

**Status:** CONFIRMED **[P]** from the Ohio temporary restraining order and the
Missouri complaint, both obtained and hashed.
**Analyst:** Aegis, for Obsidian Watch Group. **Date:** 2026-08-29.
**This finding exists because Finding 40 got it wrong.** It replaces the "four state
kratom playbook" claim with what the documents actually show, and the corrected answer
is more useful than the wrong one was.

---

## 1. Why these two cases are in a kratom case file at all

**Neither is about kratom.** Verified by word count in the primary documents:

| Document | "kratom" | "hemp" |
|---|---|---|
| Missouri complaint, *Mohemp Trade Assn v. Kehoe*, W.D. Mo. 2:26-cv-04170 | **0** | **189** |
| Ohio TRO order, *Delta Beverages v. Canepa*, N.D. Ohio 3:26-cv-01793 | **0** | 16 |

They are here for one reason: **Lifted Liquids, Inc. is a plaintiff in both**, and
Lifted Liquids is the same company whose **Roxy** 7-OH tablets appear on West
Virginia's kratom registry (Finding 39) and whose parent disclosed that scheduling
could halve its revenue (Finding 40).

**The same company litigates both its product lines the same way.** That is the
observation. It is not a kratom conspiracy and this case previously said it was.

## 2. Ohio: the state lost, and not on preemption [P]

***Delta Beverages, Inc. v. Canepa***, No. 3:26-cv-01793-JJH (N.D. Ohio), Memorandum
Opinion and Order, **filed 7 August 2026**, Judge **Jeffrey J. Helmick**.

**What Ohio SB 56 did**, per the court:

> "a company may only obtain such a license if the company's products are **sourced,
> manufactured, and distributed solely within Ohio.**"

> "Because each Plaintiff sources, manufactures, or both, its intoxicating hemp
> products outside of Ohio, **no Plaintiff is eligible to obtain a license** to
> distribute its products within Ohio's borders."

**The governing principle the court applied:**

> "'[S]tate laws offend the Commerce Clause when they seek to **build up... domestic
> commerce through burdens upon the industry and business of other States**'"

And, critically, the court's own statement of what a state **may** do:

> "**absent discrimination, 'a State may exclude from its territory, or prohibit the
> sale therein'**"

**The relief granted:**

> "I deny the motion of the State of Ohio to stay these proceedings. I grant the
> Plaintiffs' motion for a temporary restraining order, and **enjoin Defendants from
> taking any criminal, civil, administrative, or regulatory enforcement action**"

**One more thing the court recorded, and it matters:**

> "The State of Ohio's opposition brief to Plaintiffs' motion for a temporary
> restraining order is based upon its request for a stay of these proceedings and
> **does not substantively respond to Plaintiffs' arguments**."

**Ohio did not defend its own statute on the merits.** It asked for a pause.

The TRO was **extended on 19 August 2026 to 4 September 2026**, with
preliminary-injunction responses due 26 August. **An Ohio statute is enjoined as this
is written.**

## 3. Missouri: the same drafting defect [P]

***Mohemp Trade Association, Lifted Liquids, Inc. and MNG 2005, Inc. v. Mike Kehoe,
Catherine L. Hanaway and Sarah M. Wilson***, W.D. Mo. 2:26-cv-04170, complaint filed
**16 July 2026**, 32 pages, counsel Charles W. Hatfield. Defendants are Missouri's
**Governor**, **Attorney General**, and the **Director of the Department of Health and
Senior Services**, all in official capacity.

**Count II, verbatim:**

> "Under HB 2641, final hemp-derived cannabinoid products containing 0.4 milligrams of
> THC, or less, of naturally occurring, non-synthesized cannabinoids can be legally
> sold in Missouri, **but only if such products are grown and manufactured entirely in
> Missouri** and sold at a dispensary licensed with DHSS."

> "HB 2641 violates the dormant Commerce Clause of the United States Constitution
> because it **favors in-state**" producers.

> "it **discriminates against interstate commerce by insulating Missouri businesses**
> making hemp-derived cannabinoid products from out-of-state competition."

**Count I is void-for-vagueness**, alleging HB 2641 "contains hopelessly conflicting
definitions of 'marijuana,' 'hemp,' and 'hemp-derived products.'"

**Missouri wrote the same in-state-only requirement Ohio did.** No ruling yet.

## 4. Now set that against Utah [P]

Finding 37: ***Botanic Tonics v. Pherson***, D. Utah, **4 May 2026**, preliminary
injunction **DENIED**. Judge Howard C. Nielson, Jr., applying the presumption against
preemption:

> "the historic police powers of the States were not to be superseded"

**Utah's Kratom Regulation Act bans combination kratom and kava products. It bans them
for everybody.** It does not require that anything be made in Utah. There is no
in-state preference to attack.

| State | Product | The defect | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Utah** | kratom | **none. Clean prohibition, applied to all** | **Injunction DENIED** |
| **Ohio** | hemp | license only if **sourced, manufactured and distributed solely within Ohio** | **TRO GRANTED** |
| **Missouri** | hemp | legal only if **grown and manufactured entirely in Missouri** | pending |

## 5. The conclusion, and it is the useful part

**The preemption argument is not what is winning.** In Ohio it was barely reached; the
court decided on Commerce Clause discrimination, and said in terms that absent
discrimination a state may prohibit sale outright.

**What is winning is protectionism, and the states are supplying it themselves.**

Ohio and Missouri did not merely restrict an intoxicant. **They restricted it in a way
that reserved the remaining market for their own growers and manufacturers.** That
converts a public health measure into a trade barrier, and a trade barrier is the one
thing a state reliably loses on in federal court.

**Utah restricted the product and stopped there, and Utah won.**

**[I] Inference, and it is the recommendation this finding exists to make.** A state
that wants to regulate this category and survive the challenge should ban or limit the
product on its terms and **write nothing about where it is made.** The in-state
licensing clause is not the price of protecting the public. It is the thing that
loses the case.

**This is worth putting in front of the state officials being sued**, and it is worth
more to them than anything else in this case file.

## 6. What is NOT established

- **We have read the Ohio TRO order and the Missouri complaint. We have not read the
  Ohio complaint, Ohio's brief, or any Missouri response.** Ohio's substantive defense,
  if it ever files one, is not in this record.
- **A TRO is not a ruling on the merits.** It is a finding of likely success. The Ohio
  preliminary-injunction decision was due after 26 August 2026 and we do not have it.
- **The Missouri characterization of HB 2641 is the plaintiffs' own**, from their
  complaint. We have not read the statute.
- **Whether the Utah and Ohio outcomes differ because of hemp versus kratom** rather
  than because of the protectionism clause is **not established.** Hemp has an express
  federal definition under the 2018 Farm Bill that kratom has no equivalent of, and
  that alone could explain a different preemption posture. **Section 5 is the better
  explanation on this record, not a proven one.**
- The Ohio order cites *Titan Logistics*, 2026 WL 1719313, an earlier decision by the
  same judge on the same issues. **Not obtained.**
