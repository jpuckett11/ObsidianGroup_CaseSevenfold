# SEVENFOLD / FINDING 42
## Every government in this file acted on the molecule. None of them acted on a person.

**Status: [I] SYNTHESIS.** This finding introduces **no new primary evidence.** It is
an argument built on Findings 32, 34, 35, 36, 37, 39 and 41, each of which carries its
own sources. **It is labeled `[I]` throughout and should be read as reasoning, not as
a document.**
**Analyst:** Aegis, for Obsidian Watch Group. **Date:** 2026-08-29.

---

## 1. The claim this finding tests, and a correction to its first draft

The obvious reading of this case file is that elected representatives are not the
voice of the people.

**This finding was first drafted to complicate that reading**, on the ground that four
state legislatures had acted while the federal government did not, and that the
officials being sued in Findings 37, 38 and 41 were officials who moved.

**That draft was wrong, and the investigator identified the flaw immediately.** Every
action it credited was an action **about a molecule**. Utah banned a combination
product. West Virginia refused a registration. Missouri and Ohio restricted an
intoxicant. **Not one of them is help for a person with a substance use disorder.**

**Crediting a state for supply-side action is crediting it for doing the thing Finding
34 established does not work.** The corrected claim is narrower, harder, and better
supported than the one it replaces.

## 2. The federal record, which supports the bleak reading

All established elsewhere in this file:

- **Finding 32.** The only occasion eleven US Senators put this subject in writing ran
  to **537 words, twelve enforcement references, and none about treating anyone.**
- **Finding 34.** Congress removed the X-waiver, the barrier the field had named for a
  decade. **Two evaluations found no effect.** The reform was free; the ones that work
  are not, and were not attempted.
- **Finding 35.** The one treatment with a dose-response curve was capped below its
  effective dose for roughly twenty-five years, on a reading of guidance the issuing
  agency had disclaimed in 2020. **The anti-kickback safe harbour Congress ordered by
  29 December 2024 has still not issued**, twenty months late.
- **Finding 36.** The United States pleaded that kratom leaf was adulterated, litigated
  for two years and seven months, **won the contested motion, and dropped the case
  twelve days later.**
- **Finding 40.** A public company disclosed that **half or more of its revenue** turns
  on where the federal threshold falls.

**Nothing in this finding softens any of that.**

**[I] So the state record does not rebut the bleak reading. It extends it.** The
federal government scheduled one molecule and exempted the leaf. The states banned,
registered, restricted and prosecuted. **Two levels of government, four years, and not
one instrument in this file puts anything in the hands of a person trying to stop.**

**That is a sharper indictment than "representatives do not act," because it survives
the obvious rebuttal.** They do act. They act on the substance, every time, at every
level. **The person is not the object of any of it.**

## 4. [I] The mechanism: they will spend on control, not on care

The first draft of this section said the distinction was free versus expensive. **That
was too generous.** Utah spent real money defending its statute. West Virginia's
Commissioner is defending a personal damages claim. Ohio is litigating against
fourteen plaintiffs. **None of that was free.**

**The distinction that actually survives is what the money is spent on.**

| Action | Cost | Kind of spending | Taken? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Schedule one molecule, exempt the leaf | none | control | **Yes** |
| Remove a registration requirement | none | control | **Yes**, no effect (F34) |
| Ban a product category in your state | litigation, appeal, staff | **control** | **Yes**, four states |
| Refuse a registration | personal damages exposure | **control** | **Yes** (F38) |
| Prosecute retailers | prosecutors, court time | **control** | **Yes**, dozens of Ohio counties |
| Raise the CM cap to an effective dose | small. F35: the arm that worked cost $68 a head | **care** | **Not until Jan 2025, after 25 years** |
| Issue the safe harbour Congress ordered | none | **care** | **No. 20 months late** (F35) |
| Fund treatment at scale | large, recurring | **care** | **No** |

**Governments at both levels have spent freely on control and almost nothing on
care**, and the cheapest care instrument in the file, a cap that cost sixty-eight
dollars a head to make effective, took twenty-five years and is still only half fixed.

**[I] This is not an argument about budgets. It is an argument about what the money is
for.** A state will absorb a federal lawsuit to stop a product being sold and will not
absorb a line item to treat the person who was buying it.

## 5. [I] And two states handed it away

**Finding 41 is the part that is nobody's fault but the legislature's.**

Ohio wrote that a license issues only if products are "sourced, manufactured, and
distributed solely within Ohio." Missouri wrote that products are legal "only if such
products are grown and manufactured entirely in Missouri."

**Nobody required either clause.** They are not public health provisions. They reserve
the remaining market for in-state producers, and they are the reason a federal judge
enjoined Ohio's statute on 7 August 2026 while Utah's survived.

**Utah restricted the product and said nothing about where it is made. Utah is still
standing.**

**[I]** This is the one place in this case file where the failure is neither capture
nor cost. **It is legislators protecting their own state's producers inside a bill
sold as protecting the public, and thereby losing the bill.**

## 6. Why this matters to REMEDIATION

`REMEDIATION.md` argues that the fix requires government to act at a scale only
government can reach, and the rest of this file has read as evidence that government
will not.

**Tonight proves capacity and disproves nothing else.** Utah legislated, litigated and
won inside twelve months. **Government demonstrably can move fast and absorb cost when
it decides to.** It did so to stop a sale.

**[I] The revised claim, which is narrower and harder:** the problem is not that
representation does not exist, and not that government cannot act. **It is that the
entire apparatus, federal and state, is pointed at the substance, and no part of it is
pointed at the person.** Finding 35 is that in one sentence: the instrument that helps
a person was held below its working dose for twenty-five years, at a saving of
essentially nothing, while every level of government found the will and the money to
regulate what was being sold.

**REMEDIATION.md asks for the thing nobody in this file has tried.** That is now
demonstrated rather than asserted.

## 7. What is NOT established, and one of these is a real hole

- **The four states appear here because they were sued**, which selects entirely for
  states that acted. That survivorship problem was flagged in the first draft and is
  **partly closed below**, though the closing evidence is `[S]` and inconsistent.

**[S] Partial answer to the denominator, 2026-08-29.** Trade and industry trackers put
**roughly 13 to 18 states** under a Kratom Consumer Protection Act or equivalent, and
**about 10 states** with outright bans (Alabama, Arkansas, Connecticut, Indiana,
Kansas, Louisiana, North Dakota, Tennessee, Vermont, Wisconsin), with further
restrictions in California and DC. Rhode Island reversed a ban effective 1 April 2026
and moved to KCPA-style regulation. **The sources disagree with each other and none is
a government list.** Treat the range, not the numbers.

**But note what the denominator does to the argument.** If something like **twenty-five
to thirty states** have legislated on kratom, then section 3 is not a story about four
unusual states. **It is the national picture, and it makes the point worse rather than
better: dozens of legislatures found the time to regulate the substance.** This file
contains **no instance, at any level, of a corresponding measure funding treatment for
the people using it.**

**ANSWERED 2026-08-29 for one state. See Finding 43.** Utah's enacted kratom bill,
S.B. 45, states **"Money Appropriated in this Bill: None"** and never uses the word
"treatment." In the same session, **$6,161,003** of opioid treatment programs were
allowed to expire against a **$1,600,000** replacement, **$419,900** was rescinded or
cut from three named recovery organizations on 5 March 2026, and the **$543 million**
settlement principal had been locked the previous session so only interest may be
spent. **Utah does fund treatment. It funded less of it in the same months it
regulated the molecule for nothing and won a federal lawsuit about it.**

**The hedge is closed for Utah and remains open for the other KCPA states**, which is
Finding 43 section 9.
- **We do not know why the federal action took the shape it did.** Cost is our
  inference. Jurisdictional limits, agency capacity, the FDCA's structure, and ordinary
  bureaucratic lag are all available explanations and none has been excluded.
- **No claim is made that any official here acted from good motives**, only that they
  acted and were sued for it.
- **Utah's win is a preliminary injunction denial**, not a final judgment, and the
  Tenth Circuit appeal was live as of 2026-07-29.

## 8. What would test this

1. **Check whether any of those states funded treatment in the same bill or session.**
   This is now the decisive question and it is unanswered. Section 3 asserts an absence
   and an absence needs a control. **Start with the states that both enacted a KCPA and
   received opioid settlement money**, which is most of them.
2. **Ask whether the enjoined states amend.** If Ohio and Missouri strip the in-state
   clauses and re-enact, section 5 predicts the statutes survive. If they do not amend,
   that is informative about what the clauses were for.
3. **Watch the federal safe harbour.** Finding 35's missing rule is the cleanest test
   of section 4. It costs money to nobody and it still has not issued.
