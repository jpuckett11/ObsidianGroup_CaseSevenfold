# SEVENFOLD / FINDING 43
## Utah answered the question. In the same session it regulated the molecule for free, it cut treatment by three quarters.

**Status:** CONFIRMED **[P]** from the Utah Legislature's own bill PDFs and the
Executive Appropriations Committee's end-of-session budget actions, obtained and
hashed.
**Analyst:** Aegis, for Obsidian Watch Group. **Date:** 2026-08-29.
**This answers the decisive question Finding 42 section 8 named as unresolved**, and
the answer removes the hedge that finding was carrying.

---

## 1. The question

Finding 42 argued that governments act on the substance and not on the person, and
then flagged its own hole: **we had not checked whether the states passing kratom laws
also funded treatment.** An absence needs a control. Without that check, section 3 of
Finding 42 was an assertion.

**Utah was the right state to test.** It appears throughout this file: the poison
control alert of 22 July 2026, the Utah County death of 30 July, the litigation in
Findings 37 and 41.

> **CORRECTED 2026-08-29 by Finding 48.** This section is accurate about the kratom
> bill and **incomplete about the state.** S.B. 45 appropriates nothing, but Utah's
> general appropriations act, **H.B. 3**, contains **eight line items** funding S.B. 45
> to the tune of **$1,905,400 in ongoing General Fund money**, all of it to **crime
> labs, prison operations, adult probation and parole, the Board of Pardons and Parole,
> and the district courts**, while cutting the Department of Agriculture and Food by
> **$120,600**. **Utah did not regulate the molecule for free. It funded it through the
> justice system.** The conclusion of this finding survives and gets worse; see
> **Finding 48**.

## 2. The kratom side [P]

**S.B. 45, "Kratom Adjustments," 2026 General Session, signed 26 March 2026.**
Fourth Substitute, the enacted text:

> **"Money Appropriated in this Bill: None"**

**The word "treatment" appears zero times in it.** The word "kratom" appears 93.

Companion bill **H.B. 387, "Kratom Modifications"**: same. **"Money Appropriated in
this Bill: None."** "Treatment": zero. "Kratom": 70.

**On its own this proves nothing.** Regulatory bills rarely appropriate, and a state
can restrict a product in one bill and fund treatment in another. **So we checked the
budget.**

## 3. The treatment side, same session [P]

**Utah Legislature, "Opioid Settlement Programs," 2026 Legislative Session briefing:**

> "In FY24, **$7,061,033 of ongoing funding** was appropriated to opioid remediation
> programs. **This funding will expire at the end of FY26.**"

**The programs in their last year of funding, from the same document:**

| Programme | Annual amount |
|---|---|
| Treatment Project for Individuals that are Pregnant (PATH) | **$933,333** |
| Emergency Department / Urgent Care MOUD Induction | **$1,200,000** |
| **Medication for Opioid Use Disorder in Jails** | **$1,000,000** |
| Federally Qualified Health Centers: OUD Treatment Access | **$1,300,000** |
| Prevention | **$2,627,700** |
| **Total** | **$6,161,003** |

**What replaces it:**

> "The Governor's FY27 budget recommends **$1,600,000** of one-time funding be used
> from this fund for opioid remediation projects. **This represents a reduction in
> funding.**"

**$6,161,003 expiring. $1,600,000 proposed. A cut of roughly 74 percent.**

On the jail programme, the state's own document says:

> "Medications for Opioid Use Disorder (MOUD) programs provide opioid use disorder
> treatment for people who are incarcerated. **No other funding exists for these
> services.**"

## 4. And the money is not gone. It was locked. [P]

> "**$543 million (over 17 years)**"

> "Passed during the 2025 General Session, **HB 10 reclassifies the state's opioid
> settlement funding and restricts how those funds may be appropriated.** As directed
> by 2025 state law, **future funding from the Opioid Litigation Proceeds Fund is
> limited to interest earned on the fund.**"

**Utah has half a billion dollars arriving over seventeen years and has converted the
principal into a permanent fund from which only the interest may be spent.**

**The steelman, stated fairly.** That is a defensible design. An endowment funds
something forever instead of spending it in five years, and legislatures are right to
worry about cliffs. **This finding does not say the choice was corrupt or irrational.**

**What it says is what the choice does.** People with an opioid use disorder in Utah
in 2026 and 2027 are not helped by interest compounding for a person who will need it
in 2041. The jail MOUD programme, which the state says has no other funding source,
is in its last year now.

## 5. The two line items that finish the argument [P]

**Executive Appropriations Committee, "Consolidated Motions for End of Session Budget
Actions," 5 March 2026.** Two entries, both verbatim:

> "The Legislature intends that the following intent language ... which states, 'the
> Legislature intends that the Department of Health and Human Services provide direct
> award grants of **$150,000 to Fit to Recover** and **$100,000 to the School of
> Addiction Recovery** in Fiscal Year 2027 for services to individuals recovering from
> substance use disorders' **is rescinded.**"

> "Notwithstanding the intent language ... 'a direct award grant of **$269,900 to
> Jacky's Recovery Support Services** in fiscal year 2027 to increase the number of
> **rural and frontier Utahns** receiving prevention, treatment and recovery services
> for opiate use disorder' ... the Legislature intends that the Department ... provide
> a direct award grant of **$100,000**"

**Two recovery grants rescinded outright. A rural recovery grant cut from $269,900 to
$100,000.** Total removed from three named recovery organisations in one sitting:
**$419,900.**

## 6. The two columns, same legislature, same session

| Utah, 2026 General Session | Amount |
|---|---|
| Regulating the molecule (S.B. 45, H.B. 387) | **$0 appropriated** |
| Treatment programmes expiring end of FY26 | **-$6,161,003** |
| Proposed replacement | **+$1,600,000 one-time** |
| Recovery grants rescinded or cut | **-$419,900** |
| Settlement principal made unspendable (HB 10, 2025) | **$543,000,000 locked** |

**This is Finding 42's claim with a receipt.** The state did not merely decline to help
people while regulating the substance. **It regulated the substance at no cost and
reduced the help in the same session.**

## 7. Chronology, stated precisely so nobody misreads it

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| 2025 session | HB 10 locks the settlement principal |
| **2026-03-05** | **EAC rescinds and cuts the recovery grants** |
| 2026-03-26 | **S.B. 45 signed. Kratom regulated. $0 appropriated** |
| 2026-05-04 | Utah defeats the industry's injunction (Finding 37) |
| 2026-05-06 | The Act takes effect |
| 2026-07-22 | Utah Poison Control alerts on Cat's Claw products (Finding 29) |
| 2026-07-30 | Utah County death reported (Finding 29) |

**The budget decisions came before the alert and the death, not after.** Nothing here
suggests Utah cut treatment in response to anything, or that the cuts caused the death.
**They are separate events in one state in one year, and the point is only that the
same government did both.**

## 8. What this does to Finding 42

**Finding 42 section 3 said no instrument in this file puts anything in a person's
hands. That hedge can now be replaced with evidence, for one state.**

**It also corrects Finding 42 in one direction: Utah does fund treatment.** $1.6M
ongoing continues, Medicaid and federal SOR money are not in this picture at all, and
the settlement fund will pay out indefinitely. **The claim is not that Utah does
nothing. It is that Utah is doing less of it than it was, deliberately, in the same
months it found the will to regulate a product and win a federal lawsuit about it.**

## 9. What is NOT established

- **One state.** Utah is the best-documented state in this file, which is why it was
  chosen, and that is also a selection effect. **Twelve to seventeen other KCPA states
  have not been checked.**
- **The $1,600,000 is a Governor's recommendation** as described in the legislative
  briefing. Whether the enacted figure matched it has not been verified.
- **Medicaid, federal SOR grants and county-level settlement spending are outside this
  record.** Utah counties receive 50 percent of the settlement and their decisions are
  not examined here. **The state-level picture is not the whole picture.**
- **No causal claim of any kind is made** about the alert, the death, or the litigation.
