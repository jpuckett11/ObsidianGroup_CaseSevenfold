# SEVENFOLD / FINDING 44
## Tennessee's answer to a death was to criminalise the user and delete kratom from the treatment title

**Status:** CONFIRMED **[P]** from Tennessee Public Chapter 950 and House Bill 1647,
obtained and hashed. **One element is `[U]` and flagged in section 5.**
**Analyst:** Aegis, for Obsidian Watch Group. **Date:** 2026-08-29.
**Second state tested under the Finding 43 method.** It is also the investigator's
home state.

---

## 1. The law [P]

**Public Chapter No. 950**, House Bill 1649, substituted for Senate Bill 1656. Passed
the House **16 April 2026**, signed by **Governor Bill Lee**, effective **1 July 2026**.
Cited as **"Matthew Davenport's Law,"** for a Chattanooga man who died in 2024 after
an interaction between kratom and his prescription medication.

> "(a) It is an offense to **knowingly possess** Kratom."
> "(e)(1) A violation of subsection (a) is a **Class A misdemeanor**."
> "(2)(A) ... a violation of subsection (b) or (c) is a **Class C felony**."

The definition is broad:

> "'Kratom' means any part of the mitragyna speciosa plant containing the alkaloid
> mitragynine or metabolite 7-hydroxymitragynine; any substance or compound obtained
> by extraction of the mitragyna speciosa leaf; **any alkaloid or alkaloid derivative
> that has been created by chemical synthesis or biosynthetic means**; any derivative,
> analog, or other preparation of Kratom; and any derivative, analog, or other
> preparation thereof that is **substantially chemically equivalent or identical to
> Kratom.**"

**Tennessee is one of the first Southern states to ban kratom and 7-OH outright.**

## 2. The word "treatment" appears zero times in it [P]

Control: "Kratom" appears **14** times in the four-page act. **"Treatment": 0.**
**"Appropriation": 0.**

## 3. The version that did not pass was worse [P]

**House Bill 1647**, the companion, is identical in structure and harsher in penalty:

> "(e)(1) A violation of subsection (a) is **Class D felony**."
> "a violation of subsection (b) or (c) is a **Class B felony**."

**The legislature considered making simple possession of kratom a felony** and settled
on a misdemeanour. That is the range within which the debate happened.

**And the regulatory alternative was declined.** House Bill 2594, a Tennessee Kratom
Consumer Protection Act, did not advance to a floor vote in the 2025-26 session `[S]`.
**Tennessee chose prohibition over the KCPA model that thirteen to eighteen other
states adopted.**

## 4. What the act does instead of treatment [P]

It is not an empty statute. It does four things, and it is worth listing them because
none of them is care:

| Section | What it does |
|---|---|
| **2** | Creates the possession, sale and manufacture offences |
| **4** | County medical examiners **must test decedents for kratom** in suspected overdose autopsies |
| **5** | Treating physicians ordering toxicology for **suspected overdose or suspected neonatal abstinence syndrome must test for kratom** |
| **6** | Labour commissioner must add kratom to the **drug-free workplace** drug definition |

**Postmortem testing, forensic testing, and a workplace-discipline definition.**
Sections 4 and 5 will generate real data on kratom's role in overdose deaths, which
this case has wanted and which is a genuine public good. **Nothing in the act reaches
a living person who wants to stop.**

## 5. Section 3 is the one that matters [P for the deletion, U for the text]

> "SECTION 3. Tennessee Code Annotated, **Section 33-2-1404(a)(8)**, is amended by
> deleting **', including Kratom'**."

**Title 33 of the Tennessee Code is Mental Health and Substance Abuse Services.**
Chapter 2, Part 14 governs **nonresidential substitution-based treatment centers for
opiate addiction**, which is the licensing regime for methadone and buprenorphine
clinics.

**In the same act that made possession a crime, the legislature struck the words
"including Kratom" out of the statute governing opioid treatment clinics.**

**`[U]` and this is the limit of what we can say.** We could not obtain the text of
subsection (a)(8) as it stood before the deletion. Justia returned 403, Casetext 410,
FindLaw 403, LawServer 307. **We therefore do not know what requirement or permission
"including Kratom" was attached to**, and we are not going to guess at it. **What is
verified is that the phrase was in the treatment-centre statute, and that this act
removed it.**

**Obtaining that one subsection is the highest-value outstanding item on this
finding.** A Tennessee law library, a Westlaw or Lexis account, or a call to the
Tennessee Department of Mental Health and Substance Abuse Services would settle it in
minutes.

## 6. The scale, which is the investigator's point and is the right one

Finding 43 found Utah cutting **$6,161,003** of treatment to **$1,600,000**. This
finding finds Tennessee appropriating **nothing** and creating a criminal offence.

**Set those against the size of the problem.** `REMEDIATION.md` section 5 cites
*Frontiers in Public Health* 2026, DOI `10.3389/fpubh.2026.1824038`: the estimated
annual economic burden of opioid-related serious adverse events and undertreatment is
**$425.0 billion a year**, with a stated range of **$164.5B to $1,215.5B**.

**The arithmetic is not close.**

| | Amount | As a share of $425B/yr |
|---|---|---|
| Utah treatment programmes expiring | $6,161,003 | **0.0014%** |
| Utah's proposed replacement | $1,600,000 | **0.00038%** |
| Utah recovery grants rescinded 5 Mar 2026 | $419,900 | **0.0001%** |
| Tennessee's appropriation | **$0** | **0%** |

**[I]** Utah's entire opioid remediation line, before the cut, was about **fourteen
thousandths of one percent** of the annual national burden. The cut removed roughly
three quarters of that. **These are not budget decisions at the scale of the problem.
They are rounding.**

**And the $425 billion is not a proposal. It is what is already being spent**, as
consequence rather than choice, on a problem 87.6 percent of which goes untreated
(REMEDIATION s4). **The money exists. It is being spent on the aftermath.**

## 7. What is NOT established

- **The text of TCA 33-2-1404(a)(8).** Section 5. This is the gap.
- **Tennessee's separate treatment appropriations for the same session have not been
  checked.** Finding 43's method has two halves and only the first is done here.
  **Tennessee may well fund treatment elsewhere in its budget and this finding does
  not claim otherwise.**
- **HB 2594's failure to advance is `[S]`**, from secondary reporting, not from the
  legislature's own record.
- **No view is offered on whether prohibition is the right policy.** Matthew Davenport
  died and his family got a law with his name on it. **That is a real thing and this
  finding does not sneer at it.** The observation is narrower: the act criminalises
  possession, mandates testing of the dead, and removes a phrase from the treatment
  statute, and contains no provision to treat anyone.
