## 1. Why this calculation and not the usual one

Every document in this area quotes the **cost** of addiction. `REMEDIATION.md` cites
**$425.0 billion a year** (range $164.5B to $1,215.5B, DOI
`10.3389/fpubh.2026.1824038`). Finding 44 showed state budgets moving thousandths of a
percent of that.

**Nobody prices the upside.** The question here is the one a finance committee would
ask and nobody asks: **if you treated these people, what would they produce?**

## 3. The arithmetic, shown

**Step 1. Output per worker-year, derived from inputs 3 and 4 rather than assumed.**

    $702,100,000,000 / 12,500,000,000 hours   =  $56.17 per hour
    $56.17 x 2,000 hours                      =  $112,336 per worker-year

**Step 2. Workers returned, at three employment rates around the trial figure (input 7).**

| Population | at 30% | at 40% | at 50% |
|---|---|---|---|
| 914,000 | 274,200 | 365,600 | 457,000 |
| 2,000,000 | 600,000 | 800,000 | 1,000,000 |

**Step 3. Annual output added.**

| Population | at 30% | at 40% | at 50% |
|---|---|---|---|
| **914,000** | **$30.8B** | **$41.1B** | **$51.3B** |
| **2,000,000** | **$67.4B** | **$89.9B** | **$112.3B** |

**Step 4. What it costs to treat them, at NIDA's published prices.**

| | 914,000 people | 2,000,000 people |
|---|---|---|

    Treat 2,000,000 people with methadone   $13.1 billion / year

## 4. The other framing, which is AAF's not ours

Input 5 is the one that does not need our arithmetic at all.

**Opioids cost 0.6 percentage points of annual real GDP growth.** Actual growth
1999-2015 was 2.0 percent. Without the labour force loss it would have been **2.6
percent**, which is **thirty percent faster**.

On a **$30 trillion** economy, 0.6 percentage points is roughly **$180 billion per
year**, and unlike a one-off it **recurs and compounds**.

## 5. The comparison that should end the argument

**NIDA's own figures**, side by side:

| Condition | Annual cost per patient |
|---|---|
| Diabetes | $3,560 |
| **Kidney disease** | **$5,624** |
| **Opioid use disorder, methadone** | **$6,552** |

**Treating a person's opioid use disorder costs about a thousand dollars a year more
than treating their kidney disease.**

Nobody argues that dialysis is a lifestyle choice that the patient should fund
privately or go without. **Finding 44 records a state making possession of the
substance a criminal offence and appropriating nothing.** The cost difference between
those two policy postures is about **$1,000 a year per person.**

## 6. Where this estimate is weak, stated plainly

**This is the section that matters most and it is not decoration.**

- **Inputs 1 to 5 are opioid-specific.** `REMEDIATION.md`'s 42.4 million untreated is
  **all** substance use disorders. **These numbers do not scale to that population and
  this finding does not scale them.** The estimate covers opioids only.
- **The labour data is 2015 vintage.** The crisis has changed shape since, and
  stimulants are a large part of what this case file is actually about. **We have not
  found an equivalent stimulant labour-force analysis.**
- **Input 7 is one trial with 91 participants.** A 42.9 percent employment rate from a
  small therapeutic-workplace study is the weakest number here and the whole of step 2
  turns on it. **A different trial could move the answer by half.**
- **Output per worker-year (step 1) is derived from an aggregate**, and workers
  returning after years out of the labour force would very likely earn and produce
  **below** that average, at least initially. **Step 3 is therefore probably
  optimistic.**
- **Treatment does not equal employment.** Retention in MOUD is imperfect, relapse is
  common, and the model assumes a treated person stays treated. **It does not model
  drop-out.**
- **$180 billion (section 4) and $89.9 billion (step 3) are two views of overlapping
  losses. They must not be added.**
- **No offsetting costs are modelled**: treatment infrastructure, workforce, housing,
  childcare, the things Finding 35 shows are the real barriers rather than the
  medication.

**A fair reading is that the true figure is somewhere in the tens of billions per year
and that the ratio is comfortably positive.** The precision in section 3 is arithmetic
precision, not knowledge.

## 7. What survives all of that

**Even at the most pessimistic corner of this estimate**, 914,000 people, 30 percent
employment, naltrexone pricing:

    Output added   $30.8 billion / year
    Cost           $12.9 billion / year
    Ratio          2.4 : 1

**The investment returns more than it costs at every combination of assumptions
tested.** That is the finding. Not the point estimate.

**And the money already exists.** The $425 billion is not a proposal to be funded. It
is what is **already being spent** as consequence, on a problem that is
**87.6 percent untreated**. Finding 35 established that the one intervention with a
dose-response curve was capped for twenty-five years at a saving of **$68 per head**.
Finding 44 established a state appropriating **zero**.

**The argument has never been that this is unaffordable. Nobody has run the sum.**
