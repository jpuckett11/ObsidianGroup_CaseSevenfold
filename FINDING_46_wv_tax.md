# SEVENFOLD / FINDING 46
## West Virginia taxes kratom at 11 percent and spends every dollar of it on enforcing the kratom law

**Status:** CONFIRMED **[P]** from the West Virginia Code itself, `code.wvlegislature.gov`.
**Analyst:** Aegis, for Obsidian Watch Group. **Date:** 2026-08-29.
**Third state under the Finding 43 method.** Utah regulated and cut treatment.
Tennessee criminalised and appropriated nothing. **West Virginia found a third way to
do the same thing.**

---

## 1. Where the law lives [P]

**West Virginia Code Chapter 19, AGRICULTURE, Article 12F: "SELECT PLANT-BASED
DERIVATIVES REGULATION ACT: KRATOM."**

Not the health code. Not the substance abuse code. **Agriculture.** This is the same
article that produces the registry in Finding 39, the one listing thirty 7-OH products
at doses up to 210 mg.

## 2. The state taxes it [P]

**§19-12F-7. Taxation; disposition of funds:**

> "For the privilege of engaging or continuing within this state in the business of the
> retail sale of kratom or kratom products, there is hereby levied upon and collected
> from every person exercising the privilege **a privilege tax equal to 11 percent of
> the retail sales price** of kratom or kratom products sold during the reporting
> period. **Such tax is imposed in addition to all other applicable taxes.**"

And a provision worth reading twice:

> "**The tax imposed by this article shall not be added by the retailer as a separate
> charge or line item** on any sales slip, invoice, receipt, other statement, or
> memorandum of the price paid by a customer."

**The consumer pays the eleven percent and is not permitted to see it.**

## 3. Where the money goes [P]

**§19-12F-7(f):**

> "**All money received from the privilege tax** imposed under this section ... **shall
> be deposited into the Agricultural Fees Fund** created by §19-1-4c of this code."

And **§19-12F-8**, the fees:

> "Applicants ... shall pay a non-refundable application fee of **$1,500** which shall
> be deposited ... to the credit of the **Agricultural Fees Fund** ... **for the use of
> the commissioner for administering and enforcing the provisions of this article.**"

> "Processors, manufacturers, distributors, and retailer permit holders shall pay an
> **annual fee of $300**"

Finding 39 established a further **$200 per product**, against a registry of **450
products**.

**Every revenue stream in the article, the 11 percent tax and all three fees, goes to
one fund, and that fund exists to administer and enforce this article.**

## 5. What the article does build [P]

**§19-12F-9, "Cooperative enforcement agreements":**

> "the commissioner, Commissioner of the Alcohol Beverage Control Administration, and
> the Tax Commissioner ... shall agree by memorandum ... **(2) To assist each other in
> training and cooperating with State and local law enforcement to develop a state-wide
> plan** for implementing the provisions of this article; **(3) To confer among
> themselves and law enforcement about enforcement of this article on a monthly
> basis**"

**§19-12F-11, "Criminal violations; penalties":** fines of **$1,000, $5,000 and
$10,000**.

**Three state agencies, plus state and local law enforcement, meeting monthly, funded
by an eleven percent tax on the product, with a criminal penalty schedule. That is the
apparatus West Virginia built.**

## 6. Three states, three mechanisms, one result

| State | Mechanism | Money for a person |
|---|---|---|
| **Utah** (F43) | Regulate. Ban combination products | **$6,161,003 expiring, $1,600,000 proposed, $419,900 rescinded** |
| **Tennessee** (F44) | Prohibit. Possession a Class A misdemeanor | **$0 appropriated. "Treatment" deleted from the treatment-centre statute** |
| **West Virginia** (F46) | **Tax at 11 percent and licence** | **$0. Every dollar routed to enforcement** |

**[I]** These are three genuinely different policy choices, made by three different
legislatures, reaching the same place. **A state that prohibits, a state that regulates
and a state that taxes all built enforcement and none of them built care.** That is no
longer a coincidence of two.

**And West Virginia is the sharpest of the three**, because it is the only one that
raised revenue from the thing. **It found money in kratom and spent it on kratom
enforcement.**

## 7. What is NOT established

- **The Agricultural Fees Fund (§19-1-4c) has not been examined.** It is a general
  agricultural fund and may support other things. **The article restricts these
  particular revenues to administering and enforcing this article, which is what
  §19-12F-8 says on its face.**
- **We have not quantified the 11 percent.** Total West Virginia kratom retail sales
  are unknown, so the dollar value of the tax is unknown. **With 450 registered
  products this is likely material and it is worth a FOIA to the Tax Commissioner.**
- **West Virginia's separate treatment appropriations have not been checked.** As with
  Tennessee, only the statute half is done. **The state may fund treatment elsewhere
  and this finding does not claim otherwise.**
- **SB 534 (2026)** proposes a separate "Kratom Consumer Protection Fund" under the Tax
  Department. **Not obtained; the legislature's bill-text pages did not return content
  at the URLs tried.** If enacted it may change section 3 and should be checked.

## 8. Why the sweep stopped at three states

The investigator asked for the remaining kratom states. **A full sweep is currently
blocked and the blockers are recorded rather than worked around:**

- **LegiScan**, the obvious multi-state source: **HTTP 403.**
- **Open States API**, which covers all fifty legislatures: **requires a free API key**
  we do not hold. `{"detail":"Must provide API Key as ?apikey or X-API-KEY"}`
- **NASHP's state opioid settlement tracker**, which looked ideal: **the state pages
  are dated 05-17-24** and describe allocation governance rather than spending. **It
  predates everything in this case and answers a different question.** Pulled all 22
  kratom states from it before establishing that. Recorded so nobody repeats it.
- **State legislature sites individually**: workable but roughly an hour each, which is
  the estimate Finding 43 gave and it holds.

**An Open States API key would make the statute half of this tractable across all
fifty states in one pass.** That is the single thing that would unblock it.

**Three states done properly is worth more than twenty done badly**, and the three
chosen are the three this case already had documents on.
