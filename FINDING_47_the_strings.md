## 2. The Ohio question is now a Sixth Circuit question [P]

Finding 41 recorded that Judge Jeffrey J. Helmick granted the hemp TRO on 7 August 2026
citing his own earlier decision in *Titan Logistics*, 2026 WL 1719313, which we had not
obtained.

**We have now located it.** ***Titan Logistics Group LLC v. Tischler***, No.
**3:26-cv-01300** (N.D. Ohio, filed 4 June 2026). And:

> ***Titan Logistics Group LLC v. Beth Tischler***, No. **26-3648**, **United States
> Court of Appeals for the Sixth Circuit**, docketed **21 July 2026.**

**The reasoning underneath Ohio's injunction is on appeal.**

**Why that matters well beyond Ohio.** The Sixth Circuit covers **Ohio, Michigan,
Kentucky and Tennessee.** A ruling there on how far a state may go in regulating these
products would be binding law in **the state that banned kratom outright** (Finding 44).

**[I] Labeled as inference and worth stating carefully.** *Titan Logistics* and *Delta
Beverages* are hemp cases, not kratom cases, and Finding 41 was corrected once already
for blurring that line. **A Sixth Circuit holding on the Dormant Commerce Clause and
state police powers in the hemp context would not automatically decide a kratom case.**
But the doctrine is the same doctrine, and Tennessee's ban is the kind of statute it
would be measured against. **Somebody should be watching docket 26-3648.**

## 4. West Virginia's new bill does not change the answer [P]

Finding 46 flagged **SB 534 (2026)**, which proposes a "Kratom Consumer Protection
Fund," as unobtained and potentially significant. **Obtained.**

It repeals and re-enacts the whole of Article 12F. Its own summary of what it does:

> "giving rule-making authority; requiring age verification; **taxing kratom business**;
> creating an application process; permitting cooperative enforcement agreements;
> **permitting seizure of contraband**; creating criminal penalties; **establishing a
> licensing fund**; and permitting coordination."

And the disposition clause is carried over unchanged:

> "shall be **deposited into the Agricultural Fees Fund** created by §19-1-4c"

**A licensing fund, not a treatment fund. Finding 46 stands.**

## 7. What this run establishes

**The industry that sells these products is almost entirely private**, so the only
financial disclosure that exists anywhere is the one Finding 40 already found, from the
one company with a public parent. **That is not a gap we can close by looking harder.**

**And the legal question is escalating.** It began as one state's licensing refusal in
November 2025 (Finding 38) and is now before a federal court of appeals whose
jurisdiction includes the state with the harshest kratom law in the country.
