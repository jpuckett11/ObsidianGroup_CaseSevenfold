# SEVENFOLD / FINDING 48
## Utah did appropriate for its kratom law. $1,905,400 a year, to crime labs, prisons, parole and courts. It cut the regulator, and it is more than the state budgeted for all opioid remediation.

**Status:** CONFIRMED **[P]** from Utah H.B. 3 (2026 General Session), Enrolled Copy,
obtained and hashed.
**Analyst:** Aegis, for Obsidian Watch Group. **Date:** 2026-08-29.
**This corrects Finding 43**, which said Utah's kratom bill appropriated nothing. **The
bill did not. The state did, in a different bill, and the destination is the finding.**

---

## 1. The correction

Finding 43 quoted S.B. 45 accurately: **"Money Appropriated in this Bill: None."** It
then checked the opioid settlement budget and found treatment being cut, and concluded
that Utah regulated the molecule for free.

**Half of that was wrong.** Utah's appropriations ride in **H.B. 3, "Appropriations
Adjustments,"** and H.B. 3 contains **eight separate line items** whose stated purpose
is *"To implement the provisions of Kratom Adjustments (Senate Bill 45, 2026 General
Session)."*

**Regulating the molecule was not free. It was funded, and this is where the money
went.**

**How the error happened.** Finding 43 read the kratom bill and the settlement
briefing. It did not read the general appropriations act. **The method in Finding 43
section 10 had two halves and this is a third that was missing from both.**

**Utah funded its kratom law through corrections, parole, the courts and the crime
labs, and it cut the agriculture department that actually administers the registry.**

## 4. The comparison that closes Findings 43 and 44

| Utah, 2026 General Session | Amount |
|---|---|
| **Ongoing money to justice agencies to implement the kratom law** | **$1,905,400** |
| Proposed FY27 appropriation for **all** opioid remediation (F43) | **$1,600,000** |
| Opioid treatment programs expiring end FY26 (F43) | -$6,161,003 |
| Recovery grants rescinded or cut, 5 March 2026 (F43) | -$419,900 |
| Money to the agency that regulates kratom | **-$120,600** |

**Utah appropriated more recurring money to police, imprison, supervise and prosecute
around one kratom statute than it proposed for its entire opioid remediation
program.** $1,905,400 against $1,600,000. **A ratio of 1.19 to 1.**

**And the largest single line is $915,000 for Adult Probation and Parole**, which is
money to supervise people after conviction.

## 6. The pattern across three states is now exact

| State | Where the kratom money goes |
|---|---|
| **Utah** | **$1,905,400/yr to crime labs, prisons, parole and courts. Regulator cut $120,600. Treatment cut 74%** |
| **Tennessee** | Possession a Class A misdemeanor. Kratom struck from the treatment-center statute |
| **West Virginia** | **11% privilege tax, every dollar to the Agricultural Fees Fund for "administering and enforcing"** |

**[I]** Finding 42 argued that governments act on the molecule and not on the person.
**This is that argument with the general ledger attached.** Utah did not decline to
spend. **It spent $1.9 million a year, and it spent it on probation officers, prison
beds, courtrooms and a crime lab.**

## 7. What is NOT established

- **These are net adjustments in a supplemental appropriations act**, not the whole of
  those agencies' budgets. **They are what changed because of S.B. 45**, which is
  precisely what makes them attributable, but they are not total spending.
- **We have not established what the money does inside each agency.** A crime lab
  appropriation may fund the alkaloid testing the statute requires, which is arguably
  regulatory rather than punitive. **The Adult Probation and Parole line is harder to
  read that way and it is the largest.**
- **Utah's Medicaid and federal SOR treatment spending are still outside this record**,
  as Finding 43 said.
- **We have not run this check for Tennessee or West Virginia.** Their general
  appropriations acts may contain the same kind of line and **Finding 44 and Finding 46
  should be treated as incomplete until someone looks.**

## 8. What this method now makes possible

**The Open States API indexes 496 kratom bills across 49 jurisdictions**, and the
search matches bill *text*, which is why Utah's appropriations acts surfaced at all.
**That means the budget half of Finding 43's method is automatable after all**, by
searching each state's appropriations acts for the word.

**That is the sweep the investigator asked for and it is now tractable.** It was not
before this finding, because nobody knew the money was hiding in the general
appropriations act rather than the kratom bill.
