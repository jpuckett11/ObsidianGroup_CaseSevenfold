# SEVENFOLD / FINDING 49
## Tennessee and West Virginia appropriated nothing. Utah's kratom enforcement money alone would have treated 291 people, and it bought probation officers instead.

**Status:** The appropriations negatives are **CONFIRMED [P]** with a working control.
The West Virginia tax estimate is **[I] and soft**, and section 5 says how soft.
**Analyst:** Aegis, for Obsidian Watch Group. **Date:** 2026-08-29.
**Runs the Finding 48 check on Tennessee and West Virginia, and prices what all three
states collect against what treatment costs.**

---

## 1. Tennessee and West Virginia appropriated nothing [P]

Finding 48 found Utah's kratom money hiding in the general appropriations act rather
than the kratom bill. **The same search was run for Tennessee and West Virginia.**

**Neither state has an appropriations act mentioning kratom.**

**The control matters here**, because the whole finding is a negative. Open States
indexes full bill text, not just titles, and it does so **for all three states**:

| State | "Adult Probation and Parole" | "appropriation" | Appropriations acts surface? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Utah | 395 bills | 8,041 | Yes. **HB 3 and HB 8 surfaced on the kratom search** |
| **Tennessee** | 44 bills, incl. **HB 2631 / SB 2690, "Appropriations, As enacted"** | 21,440 | **Yes** |
| **West Virginia** | 58 bills, incl. **HB 5682, HB 5690, supplemental appropriations** | 6,855 | **Yes** |

**Tennessee's and West Virginia's appropriations acts are indexed and searchable, and
neither returns a kratom hit.** The instrument works. The money is not there.

## 2. What that means in Tennessee [P]

Finding 44 established that Public Chapter 950 makes possession a Class A misdemeanor
and sale a Class C felony, and appropriates nothing in the bill.

**It appropriates nothing anywhere.** And the act imposes duties that cost money:

> "**the county medical examiner shall test the decedent for the presence of Kratom**"
> in every suspected overdose autopsy (Section 4)

> "toxicology testing **must include testing for the presence of Kratom**" in suspected
> overdose **and suspected neonatal abstinence syndrome** (Section 5)

**Tennessee ordered every county medical examiner in the state to run an additional
toxicology screen on every suspected overdose death, and funded none of it.** That is
an unfunded mandate landing on county coroners, in a state that then created a new
class of misdemeanor for the courts to process.

**Compare Utah**, which appropriated **$286,800 to district courts** and **$360,000 to
crime labs** for exactly these kinds of costs (Finding 48).

## 3. What West Virginia collects [P for fees, computed]

Plus a **$1,500** one-time application fee per new applicant.

**At NIDA's $6,552 a year for methadone, West Virginia's entire kratom fee revenue
would treat fourteen people.**

## 4. The 11 percent privilege tax [I], and it is the only material number

Finding 46 established an **11 percent privilege tax on all kratom retail sales**, all
of it to the Agricultural Fees Fund for "administering and enforcing."

**West Virginia does not publish the yield. This is an estimate and it is soft.**

Scaling national market estimates by population share (WV ~1.77M of ~335M, **0.53%**):

| National estimate | Implied WV sales | 11% tax | People treated at $6,552 |
|---|---|---|---|
| $1.3B US retail (2025) | ~$6.9M | **~$756,000** | **115** |
| $2.0B US (2024, 1.7M users) | ~$10.6M | **~$1,162,000** | **177** |

**[I] Probably understated.** West Virginia has among the highest opioid-involved death
rates in the country and per-capita kratom consumption there is unlikely to sit at the
national average. **A FOIA to the West Virginia Tax Commissioner would replace this
entire section with a real number, and it should.**

## 5. The investigator's question, answered honestly

**The question was whether what these states charge and collect could pay for treating
these people. At current revenue, no, and it is not close.**

West Virginia, on the state's own prevalence arithmetic:

    Population 12+                    ~1,504,500
    With a substance use disorder       ~252,756   (16.8%, NSDUH)
    Receiving no treatment              ~221,414   (87.6%)
    Cost to treat all, at $6,552    ~$1.45 billion

    Estimated 11% tax yield             ~$1.16 million
    Share of the need covered              0.080%

**But that is the wrong comparison and it lets the states off.** Nobody claims a
kratom tax should fund an entire state's treatment system. **The right comparison is
what these states chose to do with the money they actually had.**

## 6. The right comparison

| Money | Amount | People treated at $6,552/yr | What it actually bought |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Utah, kratom enforcement, ongoing** | **$1,905,400** | **291** | crime labs, prisons, parole, courts |
| Utah, **Adult Probation and Parole line alone** | **$915,000** | **140** | supervision after conviction |
| Utah, opioid treatment **expiring** end FY26 | $6,161,003 | **940** | ends |
| Utah, proposed replacement | $1,600,000 | 244 | |
| Utah recovery grants **rescinded** 5 Mar 2026 | $419,900 | **64** | nothing |
| WV, estimated 11% tax | ~$1,162,000 | ~177 | "administering and enforcing" |
| WV, fee revenue | $92,900 | 14 | same fund |
| **Tennessee** | **$0** | **0** | unfunded mandate on coroners |

**[I] Utah's kratom enforcement appropriation, on its own, would put 291 people on
methadone for a year. The state spent it on probation officers, prison beds, courtrooms
and a crime lab.**

**The single Adult Probation and Parole line, $915,000, is 140 people.** That is money
to supervise people **after** they have been convicted, appropriated in the same
session the state let 940 people's worth of treatment expire.

**Nobody had to find new money. The money was appropriated. It went somewhere else.**

## 7. What is NOT established

- **Section 4 is an estimate built on commercial market-research figures** that vary by
  more than 50 percent between sources. **It is not evidence of anything and it is
  labelled `[I]`.** The FOIA replaces it.
- **The prevalence arithmetic in section 5** applies national NSDUH rates to West
  Virginia's population. **State-specific rates differ and WV's are likely higher.**
- **Fee revenue in section 3** assumes every registered product and registrant pays
  annually and that the registry is current. **418 rows is what the 14 August 2026 list
  contains.**
- **"People treated" throughout is medication cost only.** It excludes counselling,
  staffing, facilities and the wraparound support Finding 35 shows is the real barrier.
  **A treatment slot costs more than a prescription.** The comparison is like-for-like
  against Utah's own line items, not a claim that $6,552 buys recovery.
- **Neither Tennessee nor West Virginia's general treatment appropriations have been
  examined.** Both fund treatment through other channels not in this record.
