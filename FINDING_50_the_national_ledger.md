## 2. North Dakota, the only other state, and what it chose [P]

**Section 2** creates the **kratom regulation operating fund**:

> "a special fund in the state treasury **administered by the attorney general**. The
> fund consists of all kratom licensing fees collected under this chapter. The attorney
> general may use moneys in the fund **only for enforcement activities**"

**Section 3** is the only appropriation in the bill:

> "There is appropriated ... the sum of **$20,000** ... to the department of health and

**"Treatment" appears zero times in the bill.** Licensing fees go to the Attorney
General for enforcement only. Twenty thousand dollars goes to a webpage.

## 7. What is NOT established

- **The full package in section 4 is constructed, not observed.** No program was
  found that delivers exactly these four components at exactly this price. **The
  components are sourced; the bundle is ours.**
- **Recovery housing at six months is an assumption.** Length of stay varies enormously
  and the $1,750 midpoint is a national range, not a measured average.
- **$4,000 for vocational training is the WIOA ITA range**, not a specific program.
  `~/Desktop/OWG_TRAINING_VOUCHERS.md` shows certification can be done for far less.
- **The sweep finds appropriations acts that use the word "kratom."** A state could
  fund kratom enforcement inside a general line item that never names it, and this
  method would miss it. **The finding is that no state names it, not that no state
  spends.**
- **State treatment funding generally is outside this ledger.** Every state funds SUD
  treatment through Medicaid, SOR grants and settlement money. **The ledger is
  kratom-attributable appropriations only**, which is exactly what makes the comparison
  fair: it is what each legislature chose to spend *because of* this drug.
- **North Dakota HB 1628 is pending**, not law. Its hearing is 2 September 2026.
