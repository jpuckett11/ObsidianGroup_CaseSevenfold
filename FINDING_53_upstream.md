## 1. The corporate link, from FDA's own letter

FDA Warning Letter **CMS #709622**, dated **2025-06-25**, is addressed to: [P]

> **Recipient Name:** Stephen Vincent Sanders II
> **Recipient Title:** CEO
> Shaman Botanicals, LLC, 2405 Southwest Blvd, Kansas City, MO 64108-2344
> **support@cbdamericanshaman.com**

That is the whole link in one primary document. The CEO of Shaman Botanicals is
**Stephen Vincent Sanders II**, publicly known as Vince Sanders, and the contact
address FDA holds for the company is the American Shaman support address. This
does not rest on his own statements to reporters, and it does not rest on
secondary reporting. It is on the face of the letter.

The letter names two products:

- **7OH + Pseudoindoxyl, Green Vein, Chewable Tablets, Advanced Alkaloids (Mint Chocolate)**
- **7-OH Chewable Tablets, White Vein, Advanced Alkaloids (Lemon)**

Statutory chain, verbatim: 7-OH is a dietary ingredient under **21 U.S.C.
321(ff)(1)(F)**, and a **new dietary ingredient** under **21 U.S.C. 350b(d)**
"because, to the best of FDA's knowledge, there is no information demonstrating
that 7-OH was marketed as a dietary ingredient in the United States before October
15, 1994." Therefore adulterated under **21 U.S.C. 342(f)(1)(B)**, and prohibited
in interstate commerce under 301(a).

**That is the same subsection Botanic Tonics' amended complaint uses against
kratom itself (Finding 36, paragraph 17).** Two separate matters, one statutory
theory.

## 2. The warned product was still on shelves eight months later, out of spec

FDA warned Sanders about the **White Vein Lemon Advanced Alkaloids** tablets on
2025-06-25.

On **2026-02-13**, that same product line was recalled. openFDA enforcement
records **H-1107-2026** and **H-1108-2026**, both **Class I**, both still
**Ongoing** at pull: [P]

> Reason for recall: "A sample of the product revealed the active ingredient is in
> a quantity greater than the labeled specification."

Class I is FDA's most serious classification, reserved for a reasonable
probability of serious adverse health consequences or death.

**Volume, from the recall records:**

| Record | Presentation | Units |
|---|---|---|
| H-1108-2026 | 30-tablet bottle, 225mg per container, 7.5mg per tablet | **97,063** |
| H-1107-2026 | 2-pack, 15mg per container | 1,281 |
| H-1107-2026 | 10 packets x 2 tablets, 150mg per container | 14,491 |

**[I]** At the labeled 7.5mg per tablet, that is roughly **3.2 million tablets**,
and the recall exists because the actual content was higher than labeled. The
true mass of 7-OH recalled is unknown and is greater than the label implies.

**Do not report the 225mg or 150mg figures as tablet strengths.** They are
per-container totals. Labeled strength is **7.5mg per tablet**. This correction is
recorded because a first read of the truncated openFDA summary field got it wrong
in exactly that way.

## 4. The federal forfeiture case, and what the court actually held

**United States v. Undetermined quantities of dietary supplement products that are
labeled or otherwise appear to contain 7-hydroxymitragynine**, W.D. Mo.
**4:25-cv-00917-RK**, filed **2025-11-21**, Judge **Roseann A. Ketchmark**.
Cause **21 U.S.C. 332A**, nature of suit 690. **Not terminated.** [P]

FDA administratively detained the articles in early November 2025 at **two**
locations: **1501 Iron Street, North Kansas City** and **2461 N.W. Tullison Road,
Riverside, Missouri.** The Iron Street address is also the recalling-firm address
on both recall records.

Shaman Botanicals filed a claim and moved to dismiss on 2025-12-11. On
**2026-07-06** the court **denied** the motion (Doc. 23). From the order: [P]

> "Claimant Shaman Botanicals manufactures, holds, and distributes food products
> and dietary supplement products labeled to contain 7-OH."

> "The record before the Court... makes clear that 'Company A' as referenced in the
> civil forfeiture complaint is Claimant Shaman Botanicals."

Footnote 3 is the number this case has been chasing:

> "The attached exhibits to the complaint identifying Defendant Articles includes
> that various products contain **between 15 mg and 154 mg of 7-OH.**"

**The court also refused to consider the "Giroir Letter"**, which Shaman attached
to its motion, on the ground that it "addresses kratom and 7-OH under the
Controlled Substances Act" and so does not bear on an FDCA forfeiture. Shaman
tried to import scheduling-side arguments into a food-adulteration case and the
court declined.

## 6. What the record does NOT support

Recorded because it is the claim most likely to be repeated.

Sanders has been reported saying he white-labeled 7-OH for **six of the seven**
firms FDA warned in July 2025, and that his companies supplied roughly half the
country's 7-OH brands [S, reporting of his own statements].

**None of that is corroborated by any primary pulled here.** The FDA warning
letter to Shaman does not mention manufacturing for other brands. The forfeiture
order does not. The recall records do not. The Missouri releases do not.

What the primaries do support is narrower and still substantial: the recall
distribution list is **"14 wholesalers, 108 Distributors, 46 Company-owned stores,
2202 online customers."** [P] **108 distributors is a documented wholesale
channel.** It is not the same claim as supplying six of seven warned firms, and
the two must not be merged.

**Do not say Sanders manufactured for six of the seven warned firms.** Say he has
been reported as claiming it, and that the primary record shows a 108-distributor
wholesale channel.

Finding 09 records **NDI notification 1448, 7-Hydroxymitragynine, Shaman

**[U] The inference that Shaman filed its NDI notification in response to losing

before anyone says these events are connected.**

## 8. Instrument note, and a correction to Finding 07

Finding 07 records that the FDA warning letter pages "404 via WebFetch." That was
read as the pages being gone. **It is a fetch artifact, not a missing page.**

Retried with a browser user agent over curl, the Shaman letter returns **HTTP 200
and 37,845 bytes** and is now in evidence. A control fetch of
`fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements`, a page that certainly exists, returned
**404 by the same method**. FDA serves 404s inconsistently under automation.

**No FDA URL may be reported as missing on the strength of a 404 alone.** The
Thang Botanicals letter, which Finding 07 needs to confirm the Mitchell patent
identity, remains unretrieved. That open item stays open. It is not evidence of
absence.

## 9. What this changes

FDA's July 2025 action is recorded as seven warning letters to seven firms. On the
primaries here, at least one of those seven is a manufacturer whose CEO also
controls a national retail chain, whose warned product stayed in commerce for
another eight months, was then recalled Class I for exceeding its own label, and
was **manufactured by a Wyoming entity that no federal or state action pulled here
has named at all.**

**[I]** Enforcement has been directed at the firms whose names are on the front of
the package. The name on the back of the package has not been reached.
