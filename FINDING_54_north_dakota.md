## 2. What the only appropriation in the country actually buys

HB 1628, **SECTION 3**, quoted in full: [P]

> "APPROPRIATION - DEPARTMENT OF HEALTH AND HUMAN SERVICES - KRATOM PUBLIC HEALTH
> CAMPAIGN. There is appropriated out of any moneys in the general fund in the
> state treasury, not otherwise appropriated, **the sum of $20,000**, or so much of
> the sum as may be necessary, to the department of health and human services for
> the purpose of providing a kratom public health awareness campaign, **including
> the maintenance of kratom resources on the department's website**, for the period
> beginning with the effective date of this Act, and ending June 30, 2027."

The same bill sets fines of **ten thousand and fifteen thousand dollars**.

**[I] A single $15,000 penalty is three quarters of the entire public health
appropriation.** One enforcement action against one retailer very nearly funds, by
itself, everything the state has budgeted to inform anybody.

At the Finding 50 full treatment package of **$21,802 per person per year**,
**$20,000 does not fund one person.**

## 3. What SB 2408 schedules

Not only 7-OH. The bill names, as Schedule I: **7-hydroxymitragynine** above
0.05% dry weight or above 1 mg per article, **mitragynine pseudoindoxyl**,
**MGM-15** (dihydro-7-hydroxymitragynine), **MGM-16** (fluoro-dihydro-7-
hydroxymitragynine), and **7-acetoxymitragynine**. [P]

**[I]** This is the analog-family drafting the case predicted. The 0.05% threshold
matches the federal DEA notice. A scheduling bill written to catch the successor
compounds, with no money attached, is the pattern Finding 42 describes: the state
acts on the molecule and not on the person.

## 4. Why this one matters more than the other 72 bills

Nineteen states moved a kratom or 7-OH bill in 2026, across **73 bills** [P Open
States full text, controls run: "kratom" 496, nonsense string 0]. Only **four**
have acted since June: Massachusetts, Minnesota, New York, North Dakota.

North Dakota is the only one of the four where a scheduling bill and a funded
regulation bill are **heard in the same room on the same morning.** That makes it
the cleanest available test of the case's central remediation claim, and it is
testable on Wednesday rather than in retrospect.

**The claim:** states that act on 7-OH attach money to enforcement and nothing to
treatment. **The test:** HB 1628 carries the only kratom appropriation in the
country and it is $20,000 for a website. SB 2408 carries Schedule I penalties and
nothing at all.
