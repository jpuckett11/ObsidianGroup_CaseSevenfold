# CASE SEVENFOLD / LEADS

**Leads are not findings.** Nothing on this page is evidence and nothing here is
relied on by any finding or any filing. It exists so that things being chased are
on the record as being chased, without ever being claimed.

Every entry carries its provenance. `[U]` unverified. `[S]` secondary or
second-hand. A lead is promoted to a finding only when it can be sourced to a
primary document or a direct observation, and then it leaves this page.

---

## L-01 — Retail availability of 9-hydroxycorynantheidine

**Opened 2026-08-28. Status: OPEN, and MATERIALLY MORE CREDIBLE as of the same
day.** See `FINDING_29_the_substitution_happened.md`. Option 2 below, "a renamed or
relabelled product moving after the scheduling action," is **documented**: Utah
Poison Control reported multiple severe overdoses from smoke-shop products sold as
"Cat's Claw," and a death was linked to Buzzers-brand Cat's Claw tablets on
2026-07-30. The lead itself is still second-hand and still not relied on by any
filing, but the phenomenon it describes is now confirmed by a state agency.

**[U] Second-hand report.** The investigator reports overhearing a person state
they had purchased "9-OH" that day. Aegis has no direct contact with that person,
no product, no photograph, no receipt and no location.

**[U] Corroborating detail offered.** The same person is reported to have
described the effect as slower in onset than what they were used to. The
investigator's argument is that a layperson would not know to invent a detail
consistent with the compound's pharmacology.

**What the literature does and does not support.** It is now established that
9-hydroxycorynantheidine is a **major circulating metabolite of mitragynine in
humans** (Finding 27), and a metabolite by definition appears after the parent is
metabolised. **A delayed-onset description is therefore pharmacologically
coherent.** It is not confirmation that this specific person consumed this
specific compound.

**The first question is not whether it is real. It is what was in the package.**
"9-OH" spoken aloud in a shop could be:

1. the actual compound
2. a renamed or relabelled 7-OH product moving after the scheduling action, which
   would be **a different and arguably larger finding**
3. a distributor's marketing name for something else
4. a mishearing or misremembering of 7-OH

**What would convert this lead, in order of value:**

- a photograph of the package front and the ingredients panel
- store name, town, and date of purchase
- a receipt
- a first-person account through `deliverables/intake/`
- a certificate of analysis or independent lab result

**Checked 2026-08-28 and found nothing:** six kratom vendor sites queried directly
for "corynantheidine" and "9-OH", zero hits. General web search returns no product.
The industry's own guide page states the compound is "usually not obtainable as a
standalone product." **None of that disproves the report.** It establishes only
that the compound is not being sold through channels reachable from a browser,
which is not where the report came from.

**Not filed.** Comment `mtc-q9zv-dase` states that no products were located for
sale, and that remains accurate as to what can be documented. Filing a correction
on a second-hand report would hand the industry the cheapest possible way to
discredit the entire submission.
