Subject: Products your intake staff will not recognise as opioids — free case file, Obsidian Watch Group

Ms. K,

I run a small independent investigative group. We have spent the last month on the
federal docket concerning 7-hydroxymitragynine, and we found something that I think
matters to a community mental health centre treating chemical dependency. It is
free, it is public, and I am not selling anything.

The short version, and it is the part your intake staff will care about:

1. Products are now being sold that are engineered to substitute for 7-OH using
   compounds the federal scheduling action does not reach. One manufacturer states
   on its own homepage that it extracts an alkaloid from cat's claw "to mirror the
   effects of popular kratom products," producing "a non-kratom product with
   kratom-like effects." It is marketed as an energy and focus supplement.

2. The Utah Poison Control Center issued an alert on 22 July 2026 naming two of
   those brands, reporting multiple severe overdoses, and stating that the products
   are suspected to be adulterated with 7-OH, mitragynine pseudoindoxyl, MGM-15 or
   MGM-16, which it describes as more potent than morphine. It states plainly that
   "the product labels are misleading and do not include these drugs as
   ingredients." A death in Utah County was linked to one of these products on
   30 July.

3. The practical consequence: someone can present at your door having overdosed on
   a product whose label says "botanical extract" and lists no opioid. If intake
   asks about kratom or 7-OH by name, you will get an honest no. The person will not
   be lying and the screen will not catch it.
