Subject: Free case file on the opioid being sold in corner stores, and one ask

To whom it reaches,

I'm a licensed private investigator in Tennessee. This isn't a booking enquiry, I'm
not selling anything, and I'm not asking for money.

For the last month I've documented a product being sold as a "botanical supplement"
at gas stations, corner stores and smoke shops nationwide. It's called 7-OH. It's an
opioid. It is sold beside the register, frequently without age checks, with the
seller's own admission that it's habit-forming placed on a page the buyer never
sees. I took it myself and was physically dependent inside three weeks.

The federal government is now scheduling it. Everything I found is published free at
github.com/jpuckett11/ObsidianGroup_CaseSevenfold, and five comments are on the
federal docket. A state poison control centre has already linked one of these
products to a death.

Here is why I'm writing to you specifically.

These products concentrate where the stores are. The federal response has been to
schedule a molecule and fund nothing for the people using it. That is the same
sequence that has run before, and everybody knows how it landed and on whom. In the
government's own survey, 42.4 million Americans with a substance use disorder
received no treatment last year. 87.6 percent. Not a single federal action in this
file touches that number.

Who is actually being sold to is documented, not asserted. In a structured-interview
study of 1,274 people in treatment for substance use disorders, childhood trauma
exposure was present in 53.5 percent. A majority. These products are placed at the
register, in the stores those people actually walk into, frequently with no age
check, described as botanical wellness, with the addiction disclosure moved to a page
nobody reads. There are also reports of free samples being handed to people already
buying related products.

Every one of those is a decision someone made about where to put a product and what
to call it.

And it doesn't work on its own terms either. The month the rule was announced, the
industry moved to a compound the rule doesn't reach. One brand states on its own
homepage that it "figured out a way to extract an indole alkaloid from the Cat's Claw
plant to mirror the effects of popular kratom products." It's marketed as an energy
supplement. That's the brand a state health authority tied to a death in July.

The ask is small and you can say no to it in one line.

Would anyone on your roster be willing to point people at this? Not an endorsement,
not a partnership, no money in either direction, no obligation to agree with a word
of my analysis. A link, a mention, whatever anyone is comfortable with. Most of the
people buying this have never heard the term 7-OH and don't know the thing on the
counter is an opioid until they try to stop.

I'd add one thing. I know several artists on your roster have spoken about addiction
in their own work. I'm not going to characterise anyone's story or use anyone's name
as an example without them saying so themselves. If someone wanted to speak in their
own words, that would carry further than anything I could write.

The federal comment period closes 10 September, which is the only reason I'm writing
now rather than later.

Thank you for reading it.
