To whom it concerns at Death Row Records,

I am a licensed private investigator in Columbia, Tennessee. This is not a
booking enquiry, I am not asking for money, and I have nothing to sell.

For the last month I have been documenting the products sold as "botanical
supplements" at gas stations and smoke shops. What I found:

- 7-hydroxymitragynine, sold openly at the counter, is an opioid more potent
  than morphine. It is now being federally scheduled.
- Sellers did not stop. They moved the same class of compound into products
  relabeled "Cat's Claw," which is a different plant and is not a controlled
  substance, so the products ship to all fifty states.
- The Utah Poison Control Center issued a public alert on 22 July 2026 warning
  of life-threatening overdose, dependence and withdrawal from these products,
  and stated the labels "do not include these drugs as ingredients."
- Utah's Department of Agriculture issued a warning over mislabeled
  Buzzers-brand Cat's Claw tablets, and the Deseret News reported on 30 July
  that a death in Utah County was linked to one of them.
- One manufacturer wrote down what it was doing. Its own marketing says it
  found "a way to extract an indole alkaloid from the Cat's Claw plant to
  mirror the effects of popular kratom products," and sells it as "legal in
  all 50 states."

All of it is published free and in full, with the primary sources attached, at
github.com/jpuckett11/ObsidianGroup_CaseSevenfold. Five comments are on the
federal docket. Nothing is behind a paywall and there is nothing to sign up for.

I am writing to Death Row rather than to a health organisation on purpose.
These products are not sold in pharmacies. They are sold at the counter of the
corner store, cheap, marketed as legal and natural, in the places that already
carry the most of this. That is your audience, and almost none of them have
heard the term 7-OH. A warning from a federal agency does not reach them. A
sentence from the label they grew up on does.
