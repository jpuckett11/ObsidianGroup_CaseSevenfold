Re:  7-hydroxymitragynine, the March 18 letter to Commissioner Makary, and a gap

Senator Blackburn,

I am a constituent in Columbia. I run a small independent investigative group. We
have spent the last month on the federal 7-OH scheduling action, and I am writing
for two reasons. One is something we found that is in your favor and that you did
not ask us to look for. The other is a question I would like you to ask.

FIRST, THE PART THAT HELPS YOU

You signed the letter of 18 March 2026 to Commissioner Makary urging action on
7-OH. Ten of your colleagues signed with you.

When we started, our working theory was that the 7-OH action existed to protect
the market position of leaf-kratom companies that officials had money in. That is
a serious thing to suspect, so we tested it against primary records rather than
assuming it.

On the eleven of you, the theory failed. We queried Federal Election Commission
records by contributor employer for every kratom company and trade association we
could identify. Not one of the eleven signatories appears as a recipient of any of
it. Not you, not Chairman Crapo, not one. We have published that finding as
plainly as we published everything else, because a clean result is worth as much
as a dirty one and people are entitled to both.

I want to be accurate rather than flattering: an absence of contribution records
is not a certificate of anything, employer fields are self-reported, and we said
so in the finding. But we looked hard and we found nothing, and I would rather you
hear that from us than never hear it at all.

SECOND, THE PART WHERE I NEED YOU

Every substance the government has moved against sits on one chemical scaffold.
7-OH itself, mitragynine pseudoindoxyl, MGM-15 and MGM-16 are all structural
relatives of mitragynine.

The same plant contains a second scaffold. A compound called
9-hydroxycorynantheidine stands to corynantheidine roughly as 7-OH stands to
mitragynine, and it is covered by nothing. It appears in no federal docket
document, no lobbying disclosure and no FDA adverse event report. We ran controls
on every one of those searches.

Florida already caught it. Its emergency rule of June 2026 names the compound. The
federal instruments issued weeks later do not.

The reason this is urgent rather than academic is what we found last week. Two
companies have filed patent applications claiming how to manufacture this compound
industrially, one by cell-free enzymes and one by fermentation, both with priority
dates in late 2023. Their own filings show the compound is an intermediate on the
biosynthetic route to mitragynine, which means any facility making mitragynine
that way passes straight through it and can stop there and isolate it.

Everyone has assumed the constraint on this compound is that there is too little
of it in the leaf to matter. That assumption is already obsolete.

WHAT I AM ASKING

Not for money, not for an endorsement, and not for anything that costs your office
political capital with a colleague.

1. Ask the Department, on the record, whether its scientific and medical
   evaluation considered mu-opioid-active alkaloids of Mitragyna speciosa outside
   the mitragynine scaffold, and if it did, on what basis they were excluded.

2. Ask whether the scope of the action was set on how abundant a compound is in
   the plant, or on whether it can be manufactured. Those are different questions
   and only one of them still makes sense.

3. Ask that any subsequent notice publish the full list of alkaloids considered
   and the disposition of each, so the public can evaluate the scope rather than
   infer it.

Those three questions are already before the Department. We filed them on docket
HHS-OASH-2026-0232 as comment mtc-q9zv-dase on 28 August, alongside two earlier
comments. A comment from a private citizen can be acknowledged and shelved. The
same question from a sitting senator who already signed the March letter is
harder to shelve, and it is consistent with the position you have already taken.

Everything we have, at no cost and with no conditions.

I got into this because people I know were hurt by these products, and because the
