WHO YOU WANT

Ask for the staffer who handles health policy or FDA. Not the front desk, not
scheduling. If they are not in, ask for a name and an email and say you will send
it directly, then do that the same day.

WHAT TO SAY, THIRTY SECONDS

  "I am a constituent in Columbia. The Senator signed a letter to FDA
   Commissioner Makary in March about 7-OH. Every compound the government has
   scheduled since sits on one chemical scaffold. There is a second scaffold in
   the same plant that nothing covers, Florida already named it in an emergency
   rule, and two companies have already patented how to manufacture it. The
   comment period closes September 10. I am asking the Senator to put three
   questions to the Department, and everything I have is free and already public."

Then hand over the leave-behind and stop talking. Let them read page one.

WHAT NOT TO LEAD WITH

Not the money. Not the disclosure. Not the holding.

That material is in the letter and in the second web message they already have,
and if they ask about it, answer plainly. But it is not what gets a senator to act
and leading with it makes you the guy with a file about a colleague instead of the
guy who found a gap in a rule. The scaffold gap is checkable in an afternoon and
costs her nothing politically. That is the door.
