LEAD WITH BUZZERS. IT IS THE STRONGEST THING IN THE FILE.

The seller states its own method on its homepage: it extracts an indole alkaloid
from Cat's Claw "to mirror the effects of popular kratom products," producing "a
non-kratom product with kratom-like effects." A retailer states the reason plainly:
Cat's Claw "is not a controlled substance, so Buzzers ship nationwide."

It is sold as an energy and focus supplement. Utah Poison Control named the brand
in connection with multiple severe overdoses, and a death in Utah County was linked
to mislabelled Buzzers tablets containing MGM-15.

Sold as an upper. Named by a state health authority as causing opioid overdoses.

And "Legal in all 50 states" is on the packaging claim, while MGM-15 is Schedule I
under Utah law and Utah restricted non-leaf kratom products this year.

That is four distinct Section 5 theories in one brand, and none of them require the
Commission to take our word for anything. Every quote is captured and hashed.

WHAT ELSE MAKES THIS STRONG

Not the harm data. Agencies already have harm data.

It is the two quotes from the same domain on the same day. The sales page says
botanical supplementation; the help centre says habit-forming, addiction and
withdrawal. That is the seller's own knowledge, in their own words, placed where
buyers will not encounter it. Lead with it every time.

It also does not depend on the investigator's own account, on any conflict of
interest finding, or on any rulemaking. It is a labelling case and it stands
alone.

NEW SINCE THE FIRST DRAFT

A section on who is actually being sold to. In a structured-interview cohort of
1,274 treatment-seeking SUD patients, childhood trauma exposure was present in
53.5 percent, and comorbid adult ADHD in 14.1 percent against 2.5-5 percent in the
general adult population. A majority of the people who end up in treatment were
traumatised as children.

That matters legally, not just morally. A representation that would mislead an
ordinary consumer is treated more seriously when the foreseeable audience is a
vulnerable one, and these products are sold beside the register without age
verification.

Requested action 4 now cites Utah Poison Control's alert of 22 July 2026 and the
Utah death linked to mislabelled tablets sold under a different botanical name
(Finding 29). That moves this from "misleading marketing" to "misleading
marketing in a product class where mislabelling has now killed someone."
