# Intake administration notes
### CASE SEVENFOLD · internal · 2026-08-27

## Why the wording is neutral

Every question is deliberately non-leading. We do not ask "how quickly did you
become addicted," we ask whether anything happened when a dose was missed and how
long after starting that was. We do not ask "how bad was the withdrawal," we ask
them to describe it.

**This is not politeness, it is admissibility.** The single easiest way for
industry counsel to void this evidence is to show the instrument told respondents
what to say. Keep the wording. If you change a question, change it toward
neutrality, never away.

The same reason drives Section E on other substances. The Tampa Bay Times found
96% of kratom decedents had other substances present, and that is the industry's
primary reply to every harm claim. **Collecting it openly is what makes the
dataset survive contact.** Suppressing it would make us the thing we are
investigating.

## Safety rules, non-negotiable

1. **Crisis resources appear before any question.** Never move them lower.
2. **Never ask anyone to obtain or use a product.** The form says so explicitly.
   If someone offers to try it to help, tell them no.
3. **Do not give medical advice.** Point at SAMHSA and a doctor. That is all.
4. **Do not collect purchase sources or seller identities.** No value to us,
   real exposure to them.
5. **If a respondent discloses active crisis, stop the intake and give them the
   numbers.** The account is not more important than the person.

## Privacy handling

- Store completed forms **outside the case exhibits tree**, in a separate
  directory with restrictive permissions. Case exhibits get copied and shared;
  this material must not travel with them.
- Assign each account a **sequential code (SF-A01, SF-A02 ...)**. Use the code in
  all analysis. Keep the code-to-identity mapping in a **single separate file**.
- **Never put a respondent's name, contact, employer or town into a finding
  document.**
- Anonymised means: no name, no contact, no exact location, no employer, no
  unusual detail that identifies a person in a small community. Age band and state
  are usually safe. "A 34-year-old warehouse worker in a town of 900" is not.
- Respondents can withdraw. Honour it immediately and log the removal.

## Verification, so this is not just a pile of anecdotes

For each account, record what could be checked:

- **Product photo or receipt** if the person still has one. This is the strongest
  corroboration available and it also pins the product to a brand and a label
  claim, which matters for the 0.05% threshold question.
- **Timeline internal consistency.** Do the dates in A6, B1 and C2 agree?
- **Independent plausibility.** Does the described onset and symptom set match
  known opioid withdrawal? Note where it does and does not.
- **Duplicate check.** Same product, same timeline, same phrasing may indicate one
  person filing twice or a copied account.

Record a one-line reliability note per account. Do not discard weak accounts,
**label them.**

## Sample size and honesty about it

There is no threshold at which this becomes a study. It will never establish a
population rate and must never be described as if it does.

What a structured set of accounts **can** legitimately show:
- that dependence occurs in people with **no prior opioid dependence** (B5, B6);
- the **range and central tendency of onset timing** actually reported;
- **symptom profiles** consistent or inconsistent with opioid withdrawal;
- **product identification**, which is the part the regulatory record most lacks.

Always report the denominator. "Of 14 accounts collected, 11 reported X" is
honest. "Most users experience X" is not.

## Distribution

- Send the plaintext version. It survives every messaging app.
- Do not post it anywhere that would let a coordinated group flood it.
- Prefer people already known to the researcher or referred by a respondent.
  Referral chains are traceable and harder to poison.
- **The researcher's own account is FINDING 13 and is already recorded. It
  should be counted and labelled as the researcher's, never folded anonymously
  into the respondent set.** Doing that would be the kind of thing we criticise
  the industry for.

## Deadlines

If the count is small by 09-08, file what exists with an honest denominator. A
