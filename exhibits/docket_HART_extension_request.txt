Dear Admiral Christine:

        On behalf of Holistic Alternative Recovery Trust, a 501(c)(4) organization dedicated to promoting responsible
regulations of plant-based recovery products, we respectfully request a 60-day extension of the public comment period to
respond to the Office of the Assistant Secretary for Health's request for information (RFI), Docket No. HHS-OASH-2026-
0232, concerning the proposed threshold for the temporary placement of 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) above a specified
threshold in Schedule I of the Controlled Substances Act (CSA). We ask that the current July 31, 2026 closing date—less
than 30 days after publication in the Federal Register—be extended to no earlier than September 29, 2026.

        The RFI poses a series of highly technical and largely unprecedented questions, including how any proposed
threshold should apply across botanical source material, semi-synthetic derivatives, and finished dosage forms; what
analytical methodologies are capable of accurately measuring compliance; and how such a threshold could be
administered in practice without producing arbitrary or unintended results. These questions cannot be answered
responsibly without assembling and evaluating complex scientific, analytical, toxicological, pharmacological, and
commercial evidence from a broad range of stakeholders. Given that the Department's recommendations will directly
inform whether hundreds of thousands—if not millions—of Americans remain lawful consumers or become subject to
criminal penalties, a comment period of less than thirty days is plainly insufficient. A modest extension would
substantially improve the evidentiary record available to the Department, the Secretary of Health and Human Services,
and the Attorney General, helping ensure that any recommendation rests upon the strongest scientific and empirical
foundation available.

         For the same reasons, we respectfully urge that OASH coordinate with the Drug Enforcement Administration
(DEA) to hold the temporary scheduling order in abeyance until the evidentiary record developed through this proceeding
has been fully evaluated and incorporated into the Department's scientific and medical assessment. Emergency scheduling
is among the most consequential authorities Congress has delegated to the executive branch, permitting the government to
bypass the ordinary notice-and-comment process based upon a determination that a substance presents an "imminent
hazard to public safety." Such extraordinary authority should rest upon the most complete scientific record possible.
Proceeding before this RFI has concluded—and before the Department has had the opportunity to evaluate the
information it specifically requested from scientists, laboratories, clinicians, manufacturers, consumers, and other
interested parties—would unnecessarily risk basing a nationwide criminal prohibition on an incomplete evidentiary
record.

         This request is not intended to delay the Department's work, but rather to improve it. The questions presented in
the RFI acknowledge that important scientific, analytical, and implementation issues remain unresolved. The public
interest is best served by ensuring that those questions are answered carefully before any temporary scheduling
determination takes effect. If the scientific evidence ultimately supports the Department's proposed approach, a more
robust record will only strengthen that conclusion. If it does not, the additional information may prevent significant
unintended consequences for consumers, researchers, regulators, and law enforcement alike.

      We appreciate the Agency's consideration of this request and look forward to submitting substantive, data-driven
comments.
